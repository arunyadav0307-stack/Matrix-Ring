1. Project identity and research objective 
Working title: Quantum Error-Correcting Codes from Galois Hulls of Cyclic Codes over Noncommutative Matrix Rings. 
Research area: Algebraic Coding Theory; finite rings and matrix rings; cyclic codes; Galois duality and hulls; Gray maps; 
quantum error-correcting codes. 
Central objective: Develop a mathematically valid framework for cyclic codes over a carefully selected noncommutative 
matrix ring, define an appropriate Galois duality and hull theory, characterize the resulting hulls, and use rigorously 
justified classical-to-quantum constructions to obtain quantum code families. 
The title and scope are provisional. Phase 0 must establish whether the exact intersection is genuinely new and whether 
the proposed algebraic framework is feasible. 
2. Proposed mathematical setting — not yet fixed 
Candidate coefficient ring: 
A_t = F_q[u]/<u^t>,     u^t = 0.  
Candidate matrix ring: 
R = M_m(A_t), 
with candidate values m = 2, 3 or 4 and q = p^r. These are alternatives to investigate, not assumptions to impose. Arena 
must compare them and recommend a setting only after checking the literature, module structure, automorphisms, inner 
products and quantum-code route. 
Notation rule: use t for the nilpotency exponent and s (or ell) for the Galois automorphism parameter. Do not use k for 
both. 
Potential field automorphism: 
sigma_s(a) = a^(p^s),     a in F_q. 
Its extension to matrices may be entrywise, but Arena must prove that the extension is compatible with matrix 
multiplication and specify its action on the full coefficient ring. If q = p, the field automorphism may be trivial; this case 
must not be presented as a nontrivial Galois setting. 
Critical issue: the inner product on a noncommutative matrix ring cannot be assumed. Any proposed pairing must specify 
its domain and codomain, left/right module convention, additivity, non-degeneracy, automorphism behaviour and 
compatibility with the code operations. Candidate formulas are hypotheses to test, not established definitions.

5. Phase-by-phase execution plan 
Phase 0 — Literature review, novelty and feasibility 
Objective: Build a verified literature map covering (i) cyclic codes over matrix rings, (ii) Galois duals/hulls over rings and 
code families, (iii) quantum codes from matrix rings, and (iv) quantum codes from Galois hulls. 
Required deliverables: Annotated bibliography; comparison matrix; exact definitions used in prior work; candidate 
research gaps; feasibility report; GO/REVISE/STOP recommendation. 
Exit gate: No novelty claim without primary-source verification. Identify the closest papers and explain precisely what 
they already prove. 
Phase 1 — Select ring and establish algebraic foundations 
Objective: Compare candidate rings and fix one only after checking automorphisms, ideals/modules, left/right 
conventions and viable inner products. 
Required deliverables: Ring specification; notation sheet; lemmas on automorphism; module conventions; validated 
pairing or proof that the candidate fails. 
Exit gate: Do not proceed if the Galois automorphism is trivial or the pairing cannot be made valid for the intended 
construction.

Objective: Define cyclic codes rigorously over the selected structure and derive useful generator or module descriptions. 
Required deliverables: Definitions; structural propositions; examples; explicit small cases; computational checks.

Required deliverables: Duality theorems; proofs; generator/parity-check characterization; counterexamples where

Exit gate: Every theorem must list assumptions and prove both directions when an equivalence is claimed.

12. Important mathematical safeguards 
 A matrix ring M_m(A_t) is generally noncommutative for m greater than 1. Do not import commutative-ring cyclic-
code results without proving they apply. 
 An entrywise field automorphism on matrices must be checked against multiplication; an arbitrary matrix operation is 
not automatically a ring automorphism. 
 Do not confuse an additive subgroup, a left module, a right module, a two-sided ideal and a field-linear code. 
 Do not assume a standard dot product is suitable for matrix-valued coordinates. Specify a scalar-valued form or an 
appropriate module-valued orthogonality theory. 
 A Galois hull is an intersection C ∩ C-perp_s; its dimension and usefulness depend on the exact ambient module and 
pairing. 
 A Gray map may preserve some properties but not all. State and prove the exact result. 
 CSS construction requires appropriate classical linear codes and orthogonality/containment. A hull formula alone is 
insufficient. 
 Check the Quantum Singleton bound 2d ≤ n − K + 2 only after the quantum parameters and distance are correctly 
established. The defect is δ = n + 2 − K − 2d. 
 Do not present computational examples as general theorems. State the tested parameter range and whether 
enumeration was exhaustive. 
13. Final intended outcome 
The desired outcome is not simply a long manuscript. It is a defensible mathematical paper containing (i) a genuinely new 
and correctly defined algebraic framework, (ii) complete structural theorems on Galois duals/hulls, (iii) a valid route to 
quantum code construction, (iv) reproducible examples and parameter improvements, and (v) a literature-supported 
novelty claim. 
The project may need to change its title, ring, code family or quantum construction after Phase 0/1. Such a change is a 
sign of rigorous research, not failure. The quality of the final paper takes priority over preserving the initial title.
