## I. Finite-field and generator facts

### P01. Fixed fields, trace and norm

Set g=gcd(s,m), Q=2^g and r=m/g, using g=m when s=0. The fixed field of σ is F_Q. The fixed field E of σ² has size e=Q for odd r and e=Q² for even r, since gcd(2s,m) is respectively g or2g. For s=0 these assertions are immediate: σ=id, E=k, Q=q and r=1; no root-simplicity claim is applied to the zero polynomial T−T. For positive exponents the fixed-field statements follow from the Euclidean identity gcd(T^(2^a)−T,T^(2^b)−T)=T^(2^gcd(a,b))−T and their simple roots forming finite subfields.

σ generates Gal(k/F_Q), so Tr=1+σ+⋯+σ^(r−1) is the ordinary trace k→F_Q. It is nonzero: its polynomial, after permuting Frobenius exponents, is T+T^Q+⋯+T^(Q^(r−1)), of degree<q with leading coefficient1. It cannot vanish on all q field elements. Thus it is surjective and its kernel has F_Q-dimension r−1. Consequently

T₀=im(1+σ)=ker Tr,                                      (1)

because the inclusion follows by telescoping and both spaces have dimension r−1. This also holds at r=1, where both sides are zero. The trace pairing (a,b)↦Tr(ab) is nondegenerate: multiplication by a≠0 is bijective and Tr is nonzero.

The group k× is cyclic. One elementary justification is that the exponent of a finite subgroup of a field's multiplicative group is attained by an element (combine maximal prime-power orders), while the polynomial T^h−1 has at most h roots; hence its exponent is its order. Therefore a nonzero power map has image/kernel sizes given by the gcd of its exponent and group order. In particular the norm F_(Q²)×→F_Q×, x↦x^(Q+1), is surjective with kernel size Q+1. The analogous norm onto any finite subfield is surjective by the same calculation.

### P02. Nilpotent Nakayama and matrix packing

J³=0. Moreover B is local with radical J: write b=c+j, c∈k,j∈J. If c≠0 then b has inverse c^(−1)[1+c^(−1)j+(c^(−1)j)²]; if c=0 it is nilpotent and not a unit. Thus B/J=k and every proper ideal is contained in J. If chosen elements span M/JM, their generated submodule L satisfies M/L=J(M/L); iterating three times gives M/L=0. Conversely any generating list spans M/JM. Thus

μ_B(M)=dim_k M/JM=dim M−dim JM.                          (2)

For a right ideal of M₂(B) with column module W, each matrix generator supplies at most two B-column generators. Conversely pack a minimal list of column generators two per matrix, padding with zero if needed. Hence

μ_(M₂(B))(I_W)=ceil(μ_B(W)/2).                           (3)

The zero ideal has minimum0, though it is also principal as an ideal generated by the zero matrix.

## II. Original global pairing and the precise module family

### P03. Ring identification and right-column correspondence

Since characteristic2 gives X²−1=(X+1)², substitution w=X+1 is a ring isomorphism

R[X]/(X²−1) ≅ M₂(B),  R=M₂(k[u]/u²).

A cyclic right-linear length2 code is a right ideal: closure under right coefficients and the central cyclic shift gives closure under every right polynomial. The converse is immediate.

For any right ideal I of M₂(B), let W be its set of possible columns. Right multiplication by the matrix units extracts a column and places it in either position. Addition then assembles any two columns in W independently, so I=I_W. The same operations and scalar matrices show that W is a B-submodule. Conversely I_W is a right ideal for every W. Thus this is a **bijection**, with no GL or isomorphism quotient.

### P04. Derive the local form from all global tests

Extend coefficient Frobenius to B by fixing u,w and set θ_s(M)=σ(M)^T. It is an anti-automorphism on matrices; it is not left multiplication by θ_s on an arbitrary left action. For a word x=x₀+x₁X define x^†=θ_s(x₀)+θ_s(x₁)X^(−1). In the quotient X^(−1)=X. The constant coefficient of x^†y is precisely the original form

⟨x,y⟩_s=θ_s(x₀)y₀+θ_s(x₁)y₁.

The code also contains Xx. Since (Xx)^†=x^†X, its constant-coefficient test is the other coefficient of x^†y. Therefore orthogonality to the **cyclic code** is equivalent to x^†y=0 for every x∈C. It is not inferred from an unshifted constant coefficient alone.

Under w=X+1, inversion fixes w, so dagger becomes coefficient σ followed by transpose on M₂(B). Its (i,j) product entry is β_s(v_i,y_j)=σ(v_(1i))y_(1j)+σ(v_(2i))y_(2j), where v_i and y_j are the corresponding columns. By P03 those source columns range independently over W. Thus

C^(⊥s)=I_(D_s(W)),  C∩C^(⊥s)=I_(W∩D_s(W)).             (4)

No scalar-form replacement, Gray isometry, change of slot or side is involved.

### P05. Dimension and double-dual law

Here λ is an auxiliary Frobenius functional, **not** an assertion that the original global scalar companion [u]tr equals [z]. P04 already establishes the B-valued/global dual exactly. The equality of orthogonal submodules below is an ideal-catching argument, not equality of the two scalar forms on arbitrary words.

The functional λ:B→k extracting the coefficient of z makes (a,b)↦λ(ab) nondegenerate: each monomial 1,u,w,z pairs with its complementary monomial to z. If λ(β_s(v,y))=0 for all v∈W, then testing bv∈W for all b∈B gives λ(σ(b)β_s(v,y))=0 for every b; nondegeneracy forces β_s(v,y)=0. Hence the B-valued orthogonal equals the orthogonal for the nondegenerate k-valued σ-form λβ_s. Therefore dim D_s(W)=8−dim W. The symmetric ordinary orthogonal D₀ satisfies D₀²(W)=W and commutes with σ, so

D_s²(W)=σ²(W).                                         (5)

Explicitly, Ω_s((a,b),(c,d))=σ(a)·d+σ(b)·c has Gram matrix [[0,I₂],[I₂,0]], an invertible matrix over F₂. Its ordinary orthogonal commutes with σ, so dim D_s^Ω(T)=4−dim T and D_s^Ω²(T)=σ²(T), by the same argument. Involutivity is **not** assumed when r>2.

## III. Exhaustive structural descriptors and hull generator numbers

### P06. Scalar ideals, annihilators and minimum generators

The complete ideal list of B is

0, kz, J, B, and I_L=k(au+bw)+kz for each line L=k(a,b)≤k².

A proper ideal lies in J. If it contains a vector with a nonzero u/w layer, multiplication by u or w forces z into it. Its image in J/kz is a line or the whole k²; these give I_L or J. If the image is zero it is0 or kz. This proves completeness and distinctness.

Every I_L is generated by au+bw: one of its u/w multiples gives a nonzero multiple of z. Thus μ(I_L)=μ(kz)=μ(B)=1, μ(J)=2. Direct multiplication gives Ann(kz)=J, Ann(J)=kz and Ann(I_L)=I_L. For n∈B,

Ann(n) is 0 if n is a unit, B if n=0, J if n∈k×z,
and I_(k(n_u,n_w)) if n∈J\kz.                           (6)

For the last case the constant coordinate of an annihilator must be zero and the single relation n_u b_w+n_w b_u=0 leaves the stated radical line and arbitrary socle. All claims use characteristic2.

### P07. Unique radical descriptor, including extension data

If W⊂JB², write vectors uniquely as ua+wb+zc with a,b,c∈V. Define K={c:zc∈W} and T={(a,b):ua+wb+zc∈W for some c}. The u,w actions imply a,b∈K, so T≤K⊕K. For each (a,b)∈T its lift's c-coordinate is unique modulo K. This defines a k-linear map f:T→V/K.

Conversely any K≤V, T≤K⊕K and such f reconstruct exactly

W(K,T,f)={ua+wb+zc:(a,b)∈T, c+K=f(a,b)}.                 (7)

It is closed under u,w because those actions give zb,za with a,b∈K. Extraction recovers K,T,f, proving uniqueness. A representative for V/K is not intrinsic data; the quotient map is. This prevents double counting of lifts.

### P08. Exact radical hull, for arbitrary σ

Put P(T)=π₁(T)+π₂(T). Then JW=zP(T), dim W=dim K+dim T and

μ(W)=dim K+dim T−dim P(T).                               (8)

For radical vectors actual multiplication gives

β_s(ua+wb+zc,ua′+wb′+zc′)
 =z Ω_s((a,b),(a′,b′)),
Ω_s((a,b),(a′,b′))=σ(a)·b′+σ(b)·a′.

Let T_s=T∩D_s^Ω(T). The socle zK survives every hull, and testing the layer gives exactly

H_s(W)=W(K,T_s,f|_(T_s)),
μ(H_s(W))=dim K+dim T_s−dim P(T_s).                      (9)

Thus f affects the embedded hull but not its generator count. Ω_s is nondegenerate, with Gram matrix [[0,I₂],[I₂,0]], but generally nonreflexive.

### P09. Unique nonradical descriptor and all residue ranks

If reduction of W modulo J spans all V, then W+JB²=B²; applying P02 to B²/W gives W=B². Its dual is0.

Otherwise a nonradical W has residue a line. If that line has nonzero first coordinate, normalize a vector's unit first entry to1. There are a unique proper ideal I≤J (allowing I=J) and a unique coset t+I such that

W=B(1,t)+(0,I)={(a,ta+i):a∈B,i∈I}.                       (10)

Indeed the unit first coordinate makes the projection onto B surjective. Its kernel is0×I; a unit in I would give a second independent residue vector and force residue rank2, so I≤J. Subtracting a(1,t) from a vector of first coordinate a gives the description; replacing the normalized lift changes t only by I. For the sole vertical residue line use W=B(t,1)+(I,0), with residue t₀=0. These are disjoint deterministic charts in the original coordinates. Only an explicitly symmetric coordinate swap is used in the second calculation, not a full-GL equivalence. Thus radical, residue-line and whole-ambient cases exhaust all modules without overlap.

### P10. Exact primitive hull and its complete μ table

Write n=1+tσ(t). Testing (1,t) and (0,i), then membership in W, gives

H_s(W)={(σ(t)b,b): b∈L_s},
L_s=Ann(σ(I))∩(I:n),  (I:n)={b:nb∈I}.                  (11)

The map from L_s to the hull is a B-linear isomorphism. The second chart exchanges the coordinates. The following table is complete:

| I | μ(H_s(W)) |
|---|---|
|0|0 if n is a unit;2 if n∈k×z;1 otherwise|
|kz|2 if n₀=0;1 if n₀≠0|
|I_L|1|
|J|1|

Proof: I=0 is (6). For I=kz, Ann(σI)=J; a unit n leaves only kz, while n∈J sends all J into kz. For I_L, Ann(σI)=I_(σL); a radical n sends it into kz⊂I_L, while a unit leaves I_L∩I_(σL), either a two-dimensional principal ideal or kz. For I=J, the annihilator is kz and its product with n always lies in J. These nonzero-I hulls never vanish. Formula (11) is independent of the lift t: for j∈I, bσ(j)=0 and the norm difference times b lies in I; the displayed hull vector is unchanged. No freeness assumption is used.

### P11. Residue isotropy count d, with all endpoints

A line K≤V is σ-isotropic when σ(l)·l=0. Neither coordinate can be zero. Normalize to l=(1,a), a≠0; the condition is aσ(a)=1. It forces σ²(a)=a. On E, σ is identity for odd r and the nontrivial Q-Frobenius involution for even r. Thus a=1 in the odd case (a²=1 in characteristic2) and there are Q+1 norm-one choices in the even case by P01. Consequently

d=1 (r odd),  d=Q+1 (r even),
d=gcd(2^s+1,q−1).                                      (12)

The last expression is the root count of a^(2^s+1)=1 in k×. In particular s=0 gives d=1, without an undefined gcd convention.

### P12. Only μ3 can be nonprincipal, and exactly which inputs yield it

Primitive hulls have μ≤2 by P10. In the radical class with dim K≤1, (9) gives μ≤2: if T_s≠0 its projection sum has dimension≥1 and dim T_s≤2; if T_s=0 the value is dim K.

For K=V, f=0 and h=dim T_s≤min(dim T,4−dim T)≤2. With p=dim P(T_s), (9) is2+h−p. If h=0 or1 it is at most2. If h=2 it exceeds2 exactly when p=1. Then dim T=2,T_s=T, and T=P(T)⊕P(T)=K₀⊕K₀ for a line K₀≤V. The restriction of Ω_s to this plane is [σ(l)·l][σ(α)β′+σ(β)α′], hence vanishes exactly when K₀ is isotropic. Thus the only such W are

W=J K₀+zV, K₀ isotropic; μ(W)=μ(H)=3.

They have dim W=4 and W⊂D_s(W), so P05 gives equality W=D_s(W)=H. There are exactly d, and no hull has μ≥4. This also proves every proper hull is matrix-principal in Core A, by (3).

## IV. Count the entire input family and the easy hull strata

### P13. Grassmann counts in k⁴

There are q³+q²+q+1 lines in k⁴, by dividing q⁴−1 nonzero vectors by q−1. Hyperplanes have the same number, using nonzero linear functionals modulo scalar. Two-planes have count

(q⁴−1)(q⁴−q)/[(q²−1)(q²−q)]
 =(q²+1)(q²+q+1),                                      (13)

by counting ordered independent pairs and dividing by the number of ordered bases in a two-plane. Add dimensions0 through4 to obtain

G₄=q⁴+3q³+4q²+3q+5.                                   (14)

### P14. All radical and primitive inputs, with no quotient ambiguity

For K=0, only T=0,f=0 occurs. For dim K=1 there are q+1 choices; T has dimensions0,1,2 with counts1,q+1,1. There are q^(dim T) maps f:T→V/K. This contributes

A₀=(q+1)[1+q(q+1)+q²]=(q+1)(2q²+q+1).

For K=V, all G₄ subspaces occur and f=0. The radical total is1+A₀+G₄=q⁴+5q³+7q²+5q+7.

For each residue line in P09 and fixed I≤J, the number of t+I with its prescribed residue is |J/I|=q^(3−dim I). P06 gives the per-line sum q³+q²+(q+1)q+1. Thus primitive total=(q+1)(q³+2q²+q+1). Add the whole ambient input:

L=1+A₀+G₄+(q+1)(q³+2q²+q+1)+1
 =2q⁴+8q³+10q²+7q+9.                                  (15)

All choices are intrinsic descriptor data; P07/P09 prove uniqueness and disjointness. This proves coverage as well as the number.

### P15. N₀ and N₃

A nonzero radical W has K≠0 and surviving socle zK, so no zero hull. A primitive W with I≠0 has a nonzero hull by P10. For I=0, the hull is zero exactly when n is a unit, i.e. its residue line is anisotropic. There are q+1−d such lines and q³ lifts per line. Finally W=0 and W=B² each have hull0. Therefore

N₀=(q+1−d)q³+2,    N₃=d,    N_j=0 for j≥4.              (16)

N₃ and the last exclusion are P12, not assumptions inferred from computation.

### P16. Primitive μ1 contributions

For nonzero I the total per residue line is q²+(q+1)q+1; over all lines this is A₀. Exactly d q² cases, I=kz at an isotropic residue, have μ2 instead of1.

For I=0 and an isotropic slope a, write t=a(1+xu+yw+hz), with x,y,h arbitrary in k and aσ(a)=1. Expand n=1+tσ(t):

n_u=x+σ(x), n_w=y+σ(y),
n_z=h+σ(h)+xσ(y)+yσ(x).

It is a pure nonzero socle precisely when x,y∈F_Q and h∉F_Q: the cross term then vanishes. There are Q²(q−Q) such lifts. All other q³−Q²(q−Q) lifts have μ1 by P10. The vertical residue line is anisotropic, so no isotropic chart is lost. Hence

N₁^(primitive)=A₀+d[q³−q²−Q²(q−Q)].                      (17)

### P17. Radical K-line μ1 contributions

For dim K=1 and dim T=0/1, (9) gives μ1 whether T_s has dimension0 or1. If dim T=2, it is the full K⊕K: the layer form vanishes when K is isotropic and is nondegenerate on that two-space otherwise. Thus exactly the d q² lifts with T dimension2 and isotropic K give μ2; all remaining K-line inputs give μ1. Therefore

N₁^(K line)=A₀−d q².                                    (18)

## V. The central non-involutive point and flag counts

### P18. Reduce K=V to flags, respecting right radicals

For K=V, (9) gives μ1 exactly when T_s is a line ℓ of projection rank2 (the two vectors π₁ℓ,π₂ℓ span V). The h=0,2 alternatives cannot give1; for h=1 this is precisely p=2.

Let U count such lines ℓ with Ω_s(ℓ,ℓ)=0. For fixed ℓ, the condition ℓ⊂T_s is

ℓ⊂T⊂A_ℓ, A_ℓ={x:Ω_s(x,ℓ)=0}, dim A_ℓ=3.

The left annihilator is a k-hyperplane despite first-slot semilinearity: it is σ^(−1) applied coordinatewise to the ordinary hyperplane with nonzero coefficient vector Sℓ, S=[[0,I₂],[I₂,0]]. A field automorphism preserves subspace closure and dimension. It contains ℓ by self-isotropy. There is one T of dimension1, one of dimension3 and q+1 of dimension2 (the lines of A_ℓ/ℓ). At dimensions1/3 the right radical is exactly ℓ. At dimension2 it either is ℓ or equals T, in the latter case T is self-dual by dimension. Let F count pairs (ℓ,T) with T self-dual and ℓ⊂T of projection rank2. Every non-self-dual two-plane with μ1 has its unique ℓ and so is counted once. Thus

N₁^(K=V)=(q+3)U−F.                                    (19)

This argument never identifies left and right orthogonals for a nonreflexive form.

### P19. Independent trace-hyperplane route to all isotropic points

For a fixed a=(a₁,a₂)∈k² consider the F_Q-linear map

F_a:k²→k, b↦σ(a)·b+σ(b)·a.

If a_i≠0, set b_i=a_i x_i; its image contribution is a_iσ(a_i)T₀, by (1). If a_i=0, that coordinate contributes zero and remains a free b_i. For a≠0 with one zero coordinate, im F_a is one trace-zero hyperplane, so |ker F_a|=qQ.

For two nonzero coordinates, im F_a is the sum of two such hyperplanes. The annihilator of T₀ under the trace pairing is F_Q: inclusion follows from F_Q-linearity of Tr, and the nondegenerate pairing plus codimension1 gives equality of dimensions. Scaling replaces that annihilator by the inverse-scaled F_Q-line. Hence αT₀=βT₀ iff α/β∈F_Q×. (For r=1 both are zero and F_Q=k, so the statement still holds.) Taking t=a₂/a₁, equality is equivalent to tσ(t)∈F_Q, which is equivalent to σ²(t)=t after applying σ and cancelling σ(t). Thus exactly the e+1 projective a-directions defined over E give image dimension r−1 and kernel size qQ. All other q−e directions give two distinct hyperplanes, which span k, and kernel size q.

The two coordinate-axis a-directions were already counted in the one-nonzero-coordinate case; the ratio argument above is used only for t≠0. The a=0 case gives q² choices of b. Each nonzero a-direction has q−1 representatives; replacing a by ca and b by cb scales F_a(b) by σ(c)c, so all representatives have the same kernel size. The number Z of isotropic vectors in k⁴ is therefore

Z=q²+(e+1)(q−1)qQ+(q−e)(q−1)q
 =q³+q(q−1)[e(Q−1)+Q].                                 (20)

Subtract the zero vector and divide by q−1 (self-isotropy is homogeneous under σ(t)t) to get

I=q²+q+1+q c, c=e(Q−1)+Q.                               (21)

This is valid for non-involutive σ. It independently re-derives the working note's additive-character result without using its kernel sum.

### P20. Remove projection rank1

A rank1 projective point of V⊕V is uniquely a pair of projective lines, represented (αl,βl). Its self-pairing is

[σ(l)·l][σ(α)β+σ(β)α].

For d isotropic l-directions, all q+1 coefficient directions work. For each other l-direction, the ratio β/α must lie in F_Q, including the vertical direction: Q+1 choices. Thus rank1 isotropic points total

d(q+1)+(q+1−d)(Q+1)=(q+1)(Q+1)+d(q−Q).                  (22)

Subtract from (21) to get the displayed U in the theorem. Every nonzero point has projection rank1 or2, so none is omitted.

### P21. Self-dual two-planes descend to E

By (5), a self-dual T satisfies σ²(T)=T. Apply σ² to its unique reduced row-echelon basis: pivot positions and leading1 are unchanged, so uniqueness forces every entry into E. Therefore T is extension of a unique E-two-plane. Conversely extending an E-two-plane whose basis pairings vanish gives a k-self-dual plane, since the k-orthogonal has dimension2.

On E, σ is identity for odd r and the Q-involution for even r by P01. Thus descended planes are ordinary symplectic Lagrangians on F_Q⁴ or Hermitian self-dual planes on F_(Q²)⁴. The statement does not make σ involutive on all of k.

### P22. Numbers of descended planes and points per plane

For either the alternating or Hermitian form on the descended field, the perpendicular quotient at an isotropic line ℓ is nondegenerate: (ℓ⊥)⊥=ℓ, so the radical of ℓ⊥ is exactly ℓ. Quotienting gives dimension2 and no radical. This involutive assertion is used only over E, after P21, not on arbitrary k.

In the odd case every projective point over F_Q is Ω₀-isotropic. Through each there are Q+1 Lagrangians: quotient its perpendicular by it, obtaining a nondegenerate alternating two-space whose every line is isotropic. Every Lagrangian has Q+1 points. Hence the number of planes is

P_odd=(Q+1)(Q²+1).                                      (23)

For the even case, a nondegenerate Hermitian two-space over E has Q+1 isotropic lines. To justify this, first some vector has nonzero self-pairing: otherwise expansion forces every pairing into F_Q, and scaling one slot by a scalar outside F_Q forces it to be zero, contradicting nondegeneracy. Orthogonal splitting gives diagonal entries a,b∈F_Q×, and the equation a+b t^(Q+1)=0 has Q+1 solutions by norm surjectivity. Applied to the nondegenerate perpendicular quotient at an isotropic point, this gives Q+1 self-dual planes through it.

Formula (21), already proved without plane counts, at q=Q² and σ=Q-involution gives (Q³+1)(Q²+1) isotropic points. Each plane has Q²+1 points. Therefore

P_even=(Q+1)(Q³+1).                                    (24)

These incidence divisions count finite sets in the integers, not division by zero or by2 in the coefficient field.

### P23. Contained rank1 planes

Set Δ(a,b)=det[a b]=a₁b₂+a₂b₁. A two-dimensional matrix space all of whose elements have rank≤1 has either a common image line or a common coefficient/kernel line. Indeed if two rank-one basis matrices had independent image and coefficient factors, their sum would have rank2. Thus the contained planes have forms

{(αl,βl):α,β∈E} or {(αl,βl):l∈E²},

with respectively the image direction l fixed or the coefficient direction (α,β) fixed. The two families do not coincide as two-planes. The first is self-dual iff σ(l)·l=0; the second iff σ(α)β+σ(β)α=0. On F_Q the counts are1 and Q+1; on F_(Q²) they are Q+1 and Q+1. Hence the numbers of contained self-dual planes are

C_odd=Q+2, C_even=2(Q+1).                               (25)

### P24. Odd-order base-point incidence gives F directly

A noncontained descended plane restricts Δ to a nonzero binary quadratic over F_Q. Every projective root over k has degree≤2 over F_Q. Since [k:F_Q]=r is odd, all such roots are already in F_Q. Thus only contained planes gain rank1 points on extending scalars, gaining q−Q points each.

There are (Q+1)² rank1 points over F_Q, all symplectic-isotropic; each lies on Q+1 self-dual planes. The total base rank1 incidence is R_odd=(Q+1)³. Over k it becomes R_odd+C_odd(q−Q). Each of P_odd extended planes has q+1 points, so its complementary rank2 incidence is

F=(q+1)P_odd−[R_odd+C_odd(q−Q)]
 =q(Q³+Q²−1)−Q².                                      (26)

This re-derives F without the working note's tangent/secant enumeration.

### P25. Even-order no-external-plane lemma by graph charts

We must not assume irreducible quadratics stay irreducible across [k:E], which may be even. Instead prove that every noncontained Hermitian self-dual plane already has all its Δ-roots over E.

Project T onto its a-coordinates. If the rank is0, T=0⊕E² is contained. If the rank is1, choose a basis (l,t),(0,b₀). Then

Δ(αl,αt+βb₀)=α[α det(l,t)+β det(l,b₀)],

a product of linear forms over E, possibly a square or identically zero.

If the rank is2, T is a graph b=Ma. Its Hermitian self-duality is exactly M=σ(M)^T, so

M=[[h,x],[x^Q,j]], h,j∈F_Q, x∈E.

The restricted quadratic is

x^Q a₁²+(h+j)a₁a₂+x a₂².                               (27)
