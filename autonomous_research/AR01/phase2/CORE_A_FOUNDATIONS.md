# Core A — faithful local foundations

**AR01 mathematical working note. Complete arguments are supplied below; internal computational/proof audit is recorded separately. External novelty is NOT certified.** This is new authorized work, not an edit of MS03 or the historical blueprint. Routine Morita/duality infrastructure is not claimed as an original contribution.

## 1. Exact contract

Let k=F_(2^m), m≥2, and 0≤s<m. Put A=k[u]/(u²), R=M₂(A), S=R[X]/(X²−1), with X central. Codes are cyclic **right** ideals of S, equivalently cyclic right R-submodules of R². Set

B=A[X]/(X²−1) ≅ k[u,w]/(u²,w²), w=X+1.

The coefficient automorphism σ=σ_s raises elements of k to 2^s and fixes u,X,w. It is NOT the whole-ring power map on nilpotents. On R, θ_s(Z)=σ_s(Z)^T. The actual global pairing and right dual are

B_s(x,y)=θ_s(x₀)y₀+θ_s(x₁)y₁ ∈ R,

C^(⊥s)={y∈R² : B_s(x,y)=0 for all x∈C}.

The first slot is twisted, the tested vector is in the second slot. No involution assumption is imposed. Equivalence in this note means equality of the same embedded module under changed generators only.

## 2. Explicit local coordinates and operators

For a scalar entry f=(a₀+a₁u)+(b₀+b₁u)X, the local coordinate map is

α(f)=(a₀+b₀)+(a₁+b₁)u+b₀w+b₁uw.

Thus the local basis is **(1,u,w,uw)**. The inverse sends c₀+c_u u+c_w w+c_uw uw to

(c₀+c_w)+(c_u+c_uw)u+(c_w+c_uw u)X.

These maps are inverse k-linear maps and ring homomorphisms: substituting X=1+w gives X²−1=w²=0 in characteristic two. Apply α entrywise to matrices. This gives S≅M₂(B).

On a local coordinate row (c₀,c_u,c_w,c_uw), multiplication by u and w is respectively

U(c)=(0,c₀,0,c_w),       V(c)=(0,0,c₀,c_u).

For a vector in B² use the block-diagonal actions on its two entries. A Morita column has **8 k-coordinates**; a full matrix codeword has **16**. Do not feed a full 16-coordinate image row to an 8-coordinate Morita routine.

Frobenius applies c↦c^(2^s) to each coordinate and fixes the four basis monomials. Reversal X↦X^(−1) is **exactly the identity here**, because X²=1 in B; hence it also fixes w=X+1. This statement is specific to n=2. For n>2 it must be rederived, not copied.

## 3. Column dictionary and principality — background proved directly

For W≤B² define I_W={M∈M₂(B): every column of M belongs to W}. It is a right ideal: columns of MN are B-linear combinations of columns of M. Conversely, a right ideal I is I_W for its column module W. Multiplication by matrix units isolates and moves a column; summing isolated columns constructs every matrix in I_W. Thus no row/left-ideal substitution is used.

For g∈M₂(B), gS=I_(im g): one inclusion follows by multiplication; conversely each of the two desired columns has a preimage under g, which can be placed in a matrix. In particular

I_W is principal ⇔ μ_B(W)≤2,

including W=0, represented by g=0. If W has two generators, pack them as columns of g; if I_W=gS, its columns are generated by the two columns of g. Also dim_k I_W=2 dim_k W. These are the inherited packing facts, not new research claims.

## 4. Derive the local dual from the global one

For x(X)=x₀+x₁X and y(X)=y₀+y₁X, extend θ_s coefficientwise. The constant-X coefficient of θ_s(x(X))y(X) is

θ_s(x₀)y₀+θ_s(x₁)y₁=B_s(x,y).

Its X coefficient is θ_s(x₀)y₁+θ_s(x₁)y₀=B_s(Xx,y), since multiplication by X exchanges x₀ and x₁. A cyclic code contains Xx whenever it contains x. Therefore

[y is globally orthogonal to C]
⇔ [θ_s(x(X))y(X)=0 in M₂(B) for every x(X)∈C].

For I_W this matrix-product condition says, independently for each column z of y,

β_s(v,z):=σ_s(v₁)z₁+σ_s(v₂)z₂=0 in B for every v∈W.

Indeed the (i,j) entry of σ_s(x)^T y is β_s(column_i(x),column_j(y)), and arbitrary columns of W can be isolated in I_W. Hence, with no new form selected,

D_s(W)={z∈B²: β_s(v,z)=0 for every v∈W},

C^(⊥s)=I_(D_s(W)),       Hull_s(C)=I_(W∩D_s(W)).

For W=im g this gives D_s(W)=ker_B(σ_s(g)^T); reversal is absent only because it is the identity in this exact class. β_s is **B-valued**, σ_s-semilinear in its first slot and B-linear in its second. It is not an ordinary k-valued coordinate dot product.

This argument explicitly checks both polynomial coefficients; it does not infer a convolution identity from a single constant coefficient. It is independent of the chosen generating matrix, since the right side tests all v∈W. Changing to another presentation of the same W changes neither D_s(W) nor its hull. No ambient GL₂(B) invariance is asserted.

## 5. The actual scalar functional — no absolute-trace substitution

The retained global functional on R is Λ(Z)=[u]tr_matrix(Z)∈k. After the exact X↔w coordinate map, [u] of the constant-X coefficient of a scalar B entry is

ℓ(c₀+c_u u+c_w w+c_uw uw)=c_u+c_uw.

Thus the scalar companion of β_s is γ_s(v,z)=ℓ(β_s(v,z)). In basis (1,u,w,uw), the multiplication Gram matrix of ℓ is

J₄ = [[0,1,0,1],
      [1,0,1,0],
      [0,1,0,0],
      [1,0,0,0]].

Its determinant is 1. On B² use J₄⊕J₄ with first-slot coefficient Frobenius. On full matrix words use two column copies, with the explicit permutation/α map to the global coefficient order. This is an exact scalar form, not a coefficient-Hamming/Euclidean isometry or a physical symplectic bridge.

For a B-submodule W the γ_s and β_s orthogonals coincide. The nontrivial direction is as follows. If γ_s(v,z)=0 for all v∈W, then for every b∈B, b v∈W and

ℓ(σ_s(b)β_s(v,z))=0.

σ_s is onto, and nonsingularity of J₄ means that a∈B satisfying ℓ(ca)=0 for all c∈B must be zero. Therefore β_s(v,z)=0. This is an ideal-catching argument, not the invalid claim that nondegeneracy surjects onto an arbitrary full Hom_k(W,R).

Consequently dim_k D_s(W)=8−dim_k W. Applying σ_s to β_s(v,z)=0 and commuting scalar products gives

D_s(D_s(W))=σ_(2s)(W)

by inclusion and equal dimensions. In particular this note does not assume D_s²(W)=W for non-reflexive twists. The global double dual has the corresponding positive coefficient twist.

## 6. Radical, socle and generator calculation

Let J=(u,w)⊂B. Then J²=k·uw, J³=0 and Soc(B)=Ann_B(J)=k·uw. The last equality follows directly: u(c₀+c_u u+c_w w+c_uw uw)=c₀u+c_w uw and w times it is c₀w+c_u uw, so both vanish iff c₀=c_u=c_w=0. Thus Soc(B²)=J²B² has k-dimension 2.

For a finite B-module M, μ_B(M)=dim_k(M/JM). Here is the short nilpotent argument rather than an unreviewed freeness claim. Any d generators span M/JM, so d is at least its dimension. Conversely lifts of a basis generate a submodule N with M=N+JM. Iteration gives M=N+J^r M for all r; J³=0 gives M=N. In computations, JM=uM+wM is a **field span**, not merely a list of nonzero images. Therefore

μ_B(M)=dim_k M−dim_k(uM+wM).

This top quotient differs from embedded residue (M+JB²)/JB² and from Soc(M). It is routine computation, not a new structural-profile theorem.
