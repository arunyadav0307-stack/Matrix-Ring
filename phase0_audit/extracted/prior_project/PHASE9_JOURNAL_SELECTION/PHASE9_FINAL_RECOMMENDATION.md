## 2. Best overall: Finite Fields and their Applications

**Weighted score 4.85 / 5** (Scope 5 · Mathematical fit 5 · Recent related
work 5 · Practical 4 · Indexing 5).

### 2.1 Why

1. **The scope text names the subject.** FFA's own aims and scope list
   algebraic coding theory, cryptology and combinatorial design theory. The
   manuscript's engine — a Galois duality for cyclic codes over
   `R = M₂(A_t)` with `A_t = F_q[u]/⟨u^t⟩` — is finite-ring algebra of exactly
   the kind this journal exists for.
2. **The manuscript's lineage already runs through this venue.** Two of the 13
   cited works are FFA papers: Fan–Zhang 2017 (Galois self-dual constacyclic
   codes) and Liu–Pan 2020 (Galois hulls of constacyclic codes). The paper is
   in direct conversation with FFA's recent output.
3. **It is currently publishing in this area.** Volumes 109 (Jan 2026), 110
   (Feb 2026) and 111 (Mar 2026) all carry constacyclic/cyclic-code papers,
   including Graner's closed-form generators and a cyclotomic-coset
   construction. This is the only candidate where three consecutive 2026
   volumes were verified to contain adjacent work.
4. **A structural negative result is a credible submission here.** The
   manuscript's strongest asset is the refutation: Galois hulls with minimal
   generator number `MU_B = 3` exist, and `MU_B ≥ 3` forces `t ≥ 2` and `p | n`.
   FFA advertises "very strict refereeing standards"; a venue with that posture
   is the one place where a counterexample plus a complete theory is a normal
   article rather than a disappointment.
5. **The "no record parameters" limitation does least damage here.** The paper
   says so itself. In a venue whose editorial culture rewards improved
   parameter tables that admission is fatal; in a venue built on finite-field
   and finite-ring structure it is close to irrelevant.
6. **Length is not obviously a problem.** No page limit is stated on the
   official pages retrieved (recorded as UNVERIFIED rather than assumed
   absent). The 43 theorem environments and 13 worked examples fit a journal
   that publishes self-contained algebraic papers.
7. **The subscription route is free.** Gold OA costs USD 3 240 (excl. taxes)
   and is optional. This matters because nothing in the manuscript requires
   open access, and the author has stated no OA mandate to Phase 9.

### 2.2 What this recommendation is *not* based on

* **Not on quartile.** FFA's Q1 status (SJR 2025 0.906, Q1 in Algebra and
  Number Theory, Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Computer Science) is
  reported because the task asks for it, and it counts for 10 % of the score.
  The recommendation would stand at Q2. The decisive criteria are scope,
  mathematical fit and verified recent related work — 75 % of the weight.
* **Not on a prediction of acceptance.** See §5.

## 3. The two alternatives

### 3.1 First alternative: Designs, Codes and Cryptography (Springer)

**Weighted score 4.70 / 5** (Scope 5 · Fit 5 · Recent 4 · Practical 5 ·
Indexing 4).

**Why.** This is the field's own specialist venue and it is the strongest
*practical* choice in the set:

* **No submission or publication fee** — stated explicitly on the submission
  guidelines page. Open Choice (USD 3 290) is optional and only offered after
  acceptance.
* **Single-blind peer review**, with time to first decision usually under three
  months and online publication 3–5 weeks after acceptance — the fastest
  verified process among the top three candidates.
* **Q1 in Discrete Mathematics and Combinatorics (2025)**, with the broadest
  abstracting coverage in the set: SCIE, Scopus, zbMATH, MathSciNet, DBLP,
  EI Compendex, INSPEC and Dimensions. For a coding-theory paper,
  MathSciNet + zbMATH + DBLP coverage is the combination that matters most for
  discoverability.
* The journal's scope emphasises algebraic and geometric aspects and finite
  fields and finite geometries, so the ring-theoretic core is on topic.

**Why it is second, not first.** No specific recent hull-over-rings paper was
verified in the pages sampled (criterion C scored 4, not 5), whereas FFA had
three consecutive 2026 volumes with adjacent work. This gap may simply reflect
what the search reached — it is reported as a gap, not as a finding.

**Desk-rejection risks at DCC.** Low overall. The main ones: (a) the journal
runs open calls for papers (a WCC2026 special issue with a 2026-11-11
deadline, and a Rank-Metric Codes issue due 2027-03-31), and a submission
arriving near a special-issue deadline may be slower to route; (b) no page
limit was confirmed (UNVERIFIED); (c) the quantum parameters are a smaller
part of DCC's core identity than the coding theory is, so if the paper is
routed to a referee who reads it as a quantum paper, the "no record
parameters" line becomes more costly.

### 3.2 Second alternative: Discrete Mathematics (Elsevier)

**Weighted score 4.15 / 5** (Scope 4 · Fit 4 · Recent 5 · Practical 3 ·
Indexing 5).

**Why.** It published the single closest published neighbour found anywhere in
the search:

> Debnath, Islam, Martínez-Moro and Prakash, *Galois hulls of constacyclic
> codes over affine algebra rings*, **Discrete Mathematics 349 (2026)**, 18 pp.,
> doi 10.1016/j.disc.2025.114750.

The manuscript also cites Cao 2021 (MDS Galois hulls) from this journal, and
its scope text names coding theory, block designs, algebraic combinatorics and
matrices. Indexing is excellent: **Q1 in Discrete Mathematics and Combinatorics
continuously 2016–2025, and Q1 in Theoretical Computer Science in 2025**;
h-index 94.

**Why it is third, not first.** The venue is combinatorics-first. The
manuscript's ring-theoretic apparatus and its quantum payoff both sit off the
journal's centre of gravity, and the gold OA APC of **USD 3 530** is the
highest in the candidate set (subscription route is free).

**Desk-rejection risks at Discrete Mathematics.** (a) **Scope** — a screener
may read 43 theorem environments of noncommutative ring theory as algebra, not
discrete mathematics; this is the highest desk-stage risk of the three
recommended venues. (b) **Quantum payload** — the parameter tables are further
from the journal's core than they are at DCC.

### 3.3 Honest note: AMC is tied with Discrete Mathematics for this slot

_Advances in Mathematics of Communications scored 4.10 against Discrete
Mathematics' 4.15._ A 0.05 gap on a 5-point integer scale is noise, so this
document does not pretend to separate them. They trade off in opposite
directions:

| | Discrete Mathematics | Adv. Math. Commun. |
|---|---|---|
| coding theory in scope | peripheral to the venue's centre | **core** |
| verified 2025–26 topical cluster | one very close paper (doi 10.1016/j.disc.2025.114750) | **three papers, incl. two on hulls and EAQECCs** |
| quartile (2025) | **Q1** (Discrete Math & Comb.; TCS) | Q2 (four categories) |
| verified speed | not stated (UNVERIFIED) | **112 days median first decision** |
| gold OA APC | USD 3 530 | **UNVERIFIED** (secondary source suggests USD 1 800 — not confirmed) |

**The tie-break is the author's.** Choose **Discrete Mathematics** if
visibility and speed matter more; choose **AMC** if being read by the exact
community working on Galois hulls matters more, and if a ~4-month wait to
first decision is acceptable. If AMC is chosen, note its explicit editorial
warning that work which "does not conform" to its originality and depth
standard will be rejected — the manuscript's structural contribution should
meet that bar, but the warning is on the official page and is quoted here
because it is a real gate.
