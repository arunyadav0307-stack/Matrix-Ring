## 1. Aims and scope — VERIFIED

Verbatim material from the Guide for authors, "Aims and scope":

> "_Finite Fields and Their Applications_ is a peer-reviewed technical journal
> publishing papers in **finite field theory** as well as in applications of
> **finite fields**. As a result of applications in a wide variety of areas,
> finite fields are increasingly important in several areas of **mathematics**,
> including linear and abstract algebra, number theory and algebraic geometry,
> as well as in computer science, statistics, information theory, and
> engineering."
>
> "For cohesion … it is essential that there be a core of high-quality papers on
> theoretical aspects. In addition … the journal publishes papers on
> **computational** aspects of finite fields as well as on **algorithms** and
> complexity of finite field-related methods."
>
> "The journal also publishes papers in various applications including, but not
> limited to, **algebraic coding theory, cryptology, combinatorial design
> theory, pseudorandom number generation, and linear recurring sequences**.
> There are other areas of application to be included, but **the important
> point is that finite fields play a nontrivial role** in the theory,
> application, or algorithm."
>
> "_Finite Fields and Their Applications_ is published four times per year and
> maintains **very strict refereeing standards, accepting only those papers
> which receive excellent referee reports**."

**Accepted subject areas (VERIFIED):** finite field theory; applications of
finite fields in linear and abstract algebra, number theory, algebraic
geometry, computer science, statistics, information theory, engineering;
computational aspects; algorithms and complexity. Named application areas:
algebraic coding theory, cryptology, combinatorial design theory,
pseudorandom number generation, linear recurring sequences.

**Scope gate to keep in view:** "finite fields play a **nontrivial** role".
This is the explicit test an FFA editor applies, and it is the test this
manuscript must be framed to pass (see `PHASE10_SCOPE_AND_NOVELTY_ASSESSMENT.md`).

**Discrepancy noted (VERIFIED, both sides official).** The Guide says "published
four times per year"; the ScienceDirect issues listing shows **eight** volumes
for 2026 (Vols 109–116) and three for 2027 so far (117–119). The Guide text
appears stale. This does not affect any requirement.

## 4. Abstract — VERIFIED

> "You are required to provide a concise and factual abstract that **does not
> exceed 250 words**."

Required content: state the purpose; outline basic procedures followed; include
main findings and principal conclusions; emphasize new and important aspects.
Must **stand alone**. **Avoid references.** Avoid non-standard or uncommon
abbreviations; if essential, define at first mention.

## 5. Keywords — VERIFIED

> "You are required to provide **1 to 7 keywords** for indexing purposes."
> Written in English. Avoid multi-word keywords using "and" or "of". Use
> abbreviations only if firmly established in the field.

## 6. Highlights — VERIFIED (encouraged, not required)

> "You are **encouraged** to provide article highlights at submission."

* Separate **editable file**, uploaded in the online submission system, with
  the word "highlights" in the file name.
* **3 to 5 bullet points**, each a **maximum of 85 characters including
  spaces**.
* Should capture novel results and any new methods.

## 7. LaTeX submission requirements — VERIFIED

> "Submit files with .doc/.docx extensions for Word and **.tex for LaTeX files**.
> **A PDF is not an acceptable source file.**"
> "Format Word files in a single-column layout. Double-column formatting is
> only permitted for LaTeX submissions."

> "We **encourage** you use our [LaTeX template] when preparing a LaTeX
> submission. You will be asked to provide **all relevant editable source
> files** upon submission or revision."

Template: `els-cas-templates.zip` —

Further support: https://www.elsevier.com/latex

**Reading:** the template is *encouraged*, not mandated; the .tex source
requirement **is** mandatory.

## 8. Reference style — VERIFIED

> "This journal **does not set strict requirements on reference formatting at
> submission**."
> "References can be in **any style or format as long as the style is
> consistent**."
> "Author names, journal or book titles, chapter or article titles, year of
> publication, volume numbers, article numbers, and pagination must be
> included, where applicable."
> "**Our journal reference style will be applied to your article after
> acceptance, at proof stage.** If required, at this stage we will ask you to
> correct or supply any missing reference data."

The journal's own style when applied: numbers in square brackets in the text;
"the list of references should be arranged **alphabetically and then
numbered**"; abbreviate journal names per the LTWA.

Other reference rules:
* Any reference cited in the article must be in the reference list and vice versa.
* References cited in the abstract must be given in full.
* Every reference must correspond to a real, existing source.
* "**Where available include Digital Object Identifiers (DOIs)**."
* Unpublished results / personal communications are not recommended in the list.
* Preprint references are permitted, must be marked clearly, and must carry the
  preprint DOI.
* Data references encouraged, marked `[dataset]`.

## 9. Tables — VERIFIED

Tables must be **editable text, not images**. Cited in the text; numbered
consecutively by appearance; captions supplied; table notes below the body;
**avoid vertical rules and shading** within cells. "Use tables sparingly."

## 10. Figures and artwork — VERIFIED

Figures must be supplied as **separate files**, cited and numbered in order of
appearance; each must have a caption (brief title not displayed on the figure,
plus a description). Formats: vector drawings as EPS/PDF with fonts embedded;
photographs TIFF/JPG/PNG at ≥300 dpi; bitmapped line drawings at ≥1000 dpi.
Color appears online free of charge. Do not combine multiple images into one
file. Generative-AI rules for images are stated and restrictive.

## 11. Supplementary material — VERIFIED

> "We **encourage** the use of supplementary materials."

* Must be accurate and relevant.
* **Cite all supplementary files in the manuscript text.**
* **Submit all supplementary material at the same time as your manuscript.**
* **Include a concise caption for each supplementary file**, describing its content.
* After submission, supplementary files can only be added or replaced at the
  revision stage.
* "All supplementary material provided will appear online **exactly as
  received**. These files will not be checked, formatted, or typeset."

## 12. Research data and research-data statements — VERIFIED, and **mandatory**

> "For this journal, **Option C** instructions from our research data
> guidelines apply. This means that you are **required** to:
> * **Deposit your research data in a relevant data repository.**
> * **Provide a link to the dataset during the submission process.**
> * If sharing your research data isn't possible, **make a statement explaining
>   why** during the submission process. The statement will appear in your
>   published article."

Research data are defined to include "software, code, models, algorithms,
protocols, methods, and other relevant materials."

**This is a hard requirement with no optional reading.** Either a repository
deposit plus a link, or an explicit statement of why not, must be supplied at
submission time.

## 14. Author metadata / title page — VERIFIED

Required on the title page: **article title** (concise and informative, avoid
abbreviations and formulae where possible); **author names** (given and family
names, order matching the submission system); **affiliations** — where the work
was carried out, with lowercase superscript letters, and **the full name and
postal address of each affiliation including the country name**; **corresponding
author** clearly indicated, with an email address **that will be published**;
present/permanent address footnotes if applicable.

Related policies: a **single corresponding author** must be appointed, and
only the corresponding author's affiliation determines publishing-agreement
eligibility. **Authorship changes are generally not considered after
submission**, are allowed before acceptance only with editor approval, and are
**not allowed after acceptance**.

## 15. Peer-review model — VERIFIED

> "This journal follows a **single anonymized review process**. Your submission
> will initially be assessed by our editors. If your submission is deemed
> suitable, it will typically be sent to be reviewed by a **minimum of two
> reviewers** … Our editors decide whether to accept or reject your article."

This closes the item marked UNVERIFIED in the Phase 9 evidence register.
Editorial appeals are permitted once per submission, under Elsevier's Appeal
Policy.

## 18. Other VERIFIED items

* **Appendices:** identify as A, B, …; give separate numbering to formulae
  (Eq. (A.1), Eq. (A.2)) and to tables and figures (Table A.1; Fig. A.1).
* **Math formulae:** submit as editable text, not images; variables in italics;
  powers of e by `exp`; display equations numbered consecutively in order of
  first reference.
* **File format:** editable files only; PDF is not an acceptable source file.
* **Proof correction:** "we ask you to provide proof corrections within
  **two days**."
* **Publishing agreement:** completed after acceptance by the corresponding
  author; licence options offered for OA.
* **Article Transfer Service:** if the manuscript is judged more suitable for
  another Elsevier journal, the author may be invited to transfer it.
* **Language:** American or British English, not a combination.
* **Inclusive language, image manipulation, jurisdictional claims, sex/gender
  reporting:** policies stated; the last of these has no bearing on a pure
  mathematics paper.
