### 1. The ring, built from scratch in our library

Our `M_2(F_2)` is realised with the paper's own generators:

| object | matrix | check |
|---|---|---|
| `i` | `[[0,1],[1,0]]` | `i² = I` ✓ |
| `ω` | `[[0,1],[1,1]]` | `ω² + ω + I = 0` ✓, `iω = ω²i` ✓ |
| `u = 1 + i` | `[[1,1],[1,1]]` | `u² = 0` ✓ |

* `F_4 = {0,1,g,g²}` embeds injectively into `A` (`φ(g) = ω`); every `M ∈ A` has a unique
  form `a + ub` with `a,b ∈ F_4` ✓ (verified for all 16 elements).
* **`u` is not central:** `ωu ≠ uω` (verified).
* The multiplication rule in the coordinates `a + ub` was derived and verified for all
  256 quadruples:
  ```
  (a+ub)(c+ud) = [ac + (a+a²)d] + u[(a²+b²)d + bc + bd]
  ```
So our model *is* their ring. `[CMP]`

### 2. Exhaustive enumeration of the cyclic codes

Cyclic code = right ideal of `R_n = A[X]/<X^n − 1>` = shift-closed right `A`-submodule of
`A^n`; `X` is central; `n` odd. Ambient scanned exhaustively (`|R_1| = 16`, `|R_3| = 4096`).

| n | #cyclic codes | F₂-dimension distribution |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **5** | `0:1, 2:3, 4:1` |
| 3 | **35** | `0:1, 2:3, 4:6, 6:15, 8:6, 10:3, 12:1` |

**Independent confirmation of the number 35.** `[CMP + EST]`
Because `X` is central and `Z(M_2(F_2)) = F_2`, the Chinese Remainder decomposition must
be taken over the **centre**: `X³ − 1 = (X+1)(X²+X+1)` over `F_2[X]`, hence
```
R_3  ≅  M_2(F_2) × M_2(F_4)
```
and, by Morita, right ideals correspond to pairs of subspaces:
```
#cyclic codes = #subspaces of F_2²  ×  #subspaces of F_4²  =  5 × 7  =  35.   ✓
```
The same enumerator, run as a lattice-closure cross-check in
`galois_duality_experiments.py`, returns **35** by a completely different route
(`agreement = True`), and for `n = 1` returns **5** (`agreement = True`).

### 3. Lemma 2 at `n = 1`

> **Lemma 2 (A1).** `A[X]/(f)` is a non-commutative chain ring whose right
> `A`-submodules are only `(0)`, `(u)`, `(1)`.

Take `f = X − 1` (it divides `X^n − 1` for every odd `n`, so Lemma 2 must apply to it).
Then `A[X]/(X−1) ≅ A` as rings (`X ↦ 1` is an isomorphism since `X − 1` is central), so
the right `A`-submodules in question are exactly the right ideals of `M_2(F_2)`.

**Computed: 5 right ideals, not 3** `[CMP]`:

| dim | basis (matrices as `[[a,b],[c,d]]`) | right ideal |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | — | `(0)` |
| 2 | `[[0,0],[1,0]]`, `[[0,0],[0,1]]` | column space `⟨(0,1)⟩` |
| 2 | `[[1,0],[0,0]]`, `[[0,1],[0,0]]` | column space `⟨(1,0)⟩` |
| 2 | `[[1,0],[1,0]]`, `[[0,1],[0,1]]` | `uA = (u)` — column space `⟨(1,1)⟩` (checked: `uA = {[[a,b],[a,b]]}`) |
| 4 | all of `A` | `(1)` |

So under this reading the list `(0),(u),(1)` is **incomplete by two**. `[OPN]` The count
`3` is exactly the ideal count of the *commutative* dual-number ring `F_4[u]/<u²>` — i.e.
what one gets if `u` commuted with `F_4`, which it does not (`ωu ≠ uω`).

### 4. Theorem 1 — three readings, and the Frobenius-invariance finding

> **Theorem 1 (A1).** Let `X^n − 1 = f g h` with `f,g,h` pairwise coprime over `F_4[X]`.
> Then `C = (f h) + u(f g)` is a cyclic code, with residue and torsion generators `f h`
> and `f`, of `F_4`-dimensions `deg g` and `deg g + deg h`; conversely every cyclic code
> arises this way.

The notation `(fh) + u(fg)` admits several readings in a non-commutative ring; we tested
three:

* **R1** `C = (fh) + u(f)` — the reading consistent with their *torsion generator = f*
  statement (which is otherwise false, see below);
* **R2** `C = (fh) + u(fg)` — literal;
* **R3** `C = ⟨fh, ufg⟩` = the right ideal generated by `fh` and `ufg`.

At `n = 3` there are `3³ = 27` ordered triples `(f,g,h)` (three linear factors
`X+1, X+ω, X+ω²` over `F_4`).

| reading | #triples | #that are right ideals | #distinct codes | residue gen `= fh` | torsion gen `= f` | res dim `= deg g` | tors dim `= deg g + deg h` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | 27 | **9** | 27 | 27/27 | 27/27 | 27/27 | 27/27 |
| R2 | 27 | **4** | 27 | 27/27 | 8/27 | 27/27 | 8/27 |
| R3 | 27 | **27** | **15** | 15/27 | 15/27 | 15/27 | 15/27 |

(`n = 1`, 3 triples: R1 → 3 right ideals, R2 → 2, R3 → 3, with the four dimension
formulas holding 3/3 for R1 and R3 and 2/3 for R2.)

**The diagnostic that explains everything.** `[CMP]`
Let `σ` be the coefficientwise `F_4`-Frobenius on polynomials; it is the inner
automorphism `x ↦ i x i` of `A`, and `p ∈ F_2[X] ⟺ σ(p) = p`. Then, for **R1**:

| | right ideal | not a right ideal |
|---|---|---|
| `σ`-invariant (`f,g,h ∈ F_2[X]`) | **9** | **0** |
| not `σ`-invariant | **0** | 18 |

i.e. **`C` is a cyclic code ⟺ `f, g, h ∈ F_2[X]`** — with **no exceptions in either
direction** (at `n = 1`: 3 invariant-and-right, 0 and 0; at `n = 3`: 9, 0, 0, 18). And the
number of such triples is `3^{#F_2-irreducible factors of X^n − 1} = 3² = 9` for `n = 3`
(`X³−1 = (X+1)(X²+X+1)` over `F_2`) — not `3³ = 27`.

So the triples that work are precisely the **central** ones (coefficients in
`Z(A) = F_2`), and there are 9 of them; the paper's parametrization by `F_4`-factors
produces `3³ = 27` triples, of which 18 cannot be cyclic codes under any of our readings.

**Coverage.** `[CMP]` Even the best reading (R3) produces only **15** of the **35** cyclic
codes of length 3; R1 produces 9; R2 produces 4. The 26 codes missed by R1 are listed in
full in the log with bases; they are exactly the codes with a "column-space" component
(the `n = 1` analogue: R1 misses the two ideals with column space `⟨(0,1)⟩`, `⟨(1,0)⟩`,
both of which *are* cyclic codes). If Theorem 1 asserts that *every* cyclic code arises
this way, then under our reading it does not hold at `n = 3`; if "cyclic code" means
something else in their convention, there is no contradiction — see §6.

### 5. Duality: Lemma 4 and Theorem 2 at `n = 3`

Two forms were tested on all 35 codes and all 27 triples:

* **their Euclidean form** `Σ_j x_j y_j`;
* **the transpose form** `Σ_j x_j^T y_j` (this is our `θ`-pairing at `s = 0`).

| test | Euclidean `Σ x_j y_j` | transpose `Σ x_j^T y_j` |
|---|---|---|
| `dim C + dim C^⊥` over the 35 codes | **not constant**: `{6: 15, 8: 10, 10: 6, 12: 4}` | **exactly 12 for all 35** |
| self-dual codes | **0** | **3** (`#3, #6, #10`, all `dim 6`, `|C| = 64`) |
| Lemma 4 (`C^⊥` built from `(f',g',h') = (g*,f*,h*)`) | 3/27 (3/9 on the 9 central triples) | 5/27 (5/9) |

> **Theorem 2 (A1).** `C` is Euclidean self-dual iff `h = h*` and `g = f*` (`p*` = monic
> reciprocal).

Three triples satisfy `h = h*` and `g = f*`:

| `(f,g,h)` | `dim C` | `C = C^⊥` (Euclidean) | `C = C^⊥` (transpose) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `f = X+ω`, `g = X+ω²`, `h = X+1` | 6 | False | False |
| `f = X+ω²`, `g = X+ω`, `h = X+1` | 6 | False | False |
| `f = 1`, `g = 1`, `h = X³+1` | 6 | False | **True** |

And **none of the three self-dual codes we computed** (`#3, #6, #10`) is produced by
reading R1 of Theorem 1.

Consistent with §4: the Euclidean form `Σ x_j y_j` is degenerate on the right module
`R_3` (the dimension sum is not even constant), while the transpose form satisfies the
size formula `dim C + dim C^⊥ = 12` for all 35 codes. This is the same phenomenon our
Task-1/2 work explains structurally: for **right** modules the correct pairing is
`Σ θ_s(x_i) y_i`, and at `s = 0` that is `Σ x_i^T y_i`.

### 6. Candidate reconciling conventions (all `[OPN]`)

1. **Side convention.** A1 may parametrise *left* ideals. (Our count is unchanged at 35
   by the transpose anti-isomorphism, so this alone does not explain 27 vs 35, but it
   would change which readings of `(fh) + u(fg)` are legitimate.)
2. **Meaning of `(p)`.** `(fh)` may denote the *right* ideal generated by `fh` — our R3 —
   rather than the set of multiples in the ambient, or a `u`-torsion summand; different
   readings give 9, 4 or 15 codes.
3. **Chain-ring convention in Lemma 2.** The count 3 matches the commutative ring
   `F_4[u]/<u²>`; if their `A[X]/(f)` is meant to be a chain ring in that sense, then
   `u` must commute with `F_4` in their coordinate conventions, which is false in
   `M_2(F_2)` as we build it (`ωu ≠ uω`).
4. **Factorization base field.** `X^n − 1` is factored in `F_4[X]` in A1, but `F_4 ⊂ A`
   is **not central**, so the CRT/product decomposition of `R_n` over `F_4`-factors is
   not a decomposition of right `A`-modules in the usual sense. Factoring over the
   centre `F_2[X]` gives `M_2(F_2) × M_2(F_4)` and the count `5 × 7 = 35`, which matches
   both of our enumerations.

We regard (4), together with the Frobenius-invariance theorem of §4, as the most likely
explanation, but we do **not** assert it as a defect of the paper without the authors'
conventions in hand.

### 7. What would settle it

1. Check whether `A[X]/(X−1) ≅ A` is intended to fall under Lemma 2; if yes, the list
   `(0),(u),(1)` misses two right ideals (§3) — a `≤ 5`-element computation.
2. Check whether their `(fh)` means a right, left or two-sided generated object, and
   whether `X^n − 1` is factored in `F_2[X]` or `F_4[X]` in Theorem 1.
3. Check the ambient for their Euclidean form: is `C^⊥` taken inside `A^n` with the
   `F_2`-bilinear form `Σ tr(x_j y_j)`, `Σ x_j y_j`, or `Σ x_j^T y_j`? Only the last
   satisfies `dim C + dim C^⊥ = 12` for all 35 codes (§5).

Until (1)–(3) are settled, this project will treat A1 as **"closest paper, right-ideal
convention confirmed, structure theory not usable as a black box"** and will re-derive
whatever it needs. Concretely: **for `t = 1` we will use the central (Morita/CRT over
`F_q[X]`) enumeration, which is validated by two independent computations, rather than
import A1's generator description.**
