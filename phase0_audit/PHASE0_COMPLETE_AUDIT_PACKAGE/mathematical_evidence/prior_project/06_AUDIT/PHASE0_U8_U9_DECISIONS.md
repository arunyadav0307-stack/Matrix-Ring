# U8 — The Alahmadi (A1) convention

## 1. The recorded statement and why it cannot be right as written

The statement recorded in the package is, in substance:

> Let `ℓ > 0` be an integer and `j ∈ {0,1,2}`. [The relevant cyclic codes over
> `M_2(F_2)` are generated by] (1) `x²+x+1` if `j = 0`; (2) `(x³+x+1)(x³+x²+1)` if
> `j = 1`; (3) `(x+1)(x³+x+1)(x³+x²+1)` if `j = 2` … the third case enumerates all 8.

**Internal inconsistency (F-01).** A generator of a cyclic code of length `n` must divide
`X^n − 1`. Over `F_2`:

* `X² + X + 1 ∣ X³ − 1` (indeed `X³ − 1 = (X+1)(X²+X+1)`) and `X²+X+1 ∤ X⁷ − 1`
  (its roots have order 3);
* `X³ + X + 1` and `X³ + X² + 1 ∣ X⁷ − 1` (indeed `X⁷ − 1 = (X+1)(X³+X+1)(X³+X²+1)`)
  and neither divides `X³ − 1`;
* `gcd(3, 7) = 1`, so no `n` has all three as divisors of `X^n − 1`
  (`X^n − 1` is divisible by `X²+X+1` iff `3 | n`, and by `X⁷+1` iff `7 | n`; the union
  of the two lists is only possible at `n = 21`, where `X²¹ − 1` has **six** irreducible
  factors and hence 64 divisors, not 8).

So the recorded statement is a **mis-transcription**: it splices material from at least
two lengths (`n = 3` and `n = 7`). The paper itself is paywalled and could not be
retrieved from this sandbox.

## 2. What the numbers "8" and "35" actually are

Two *different* families are in play, and the package's own convention (A1's Sec. 2:
"`R_n = A[X]/(X^n−1)` whose RIGHT SIDED IDEALS represent cyclic codes of length n")
supports only the first:

**(I) Right-ideal convention (ours).** `R_3 = M_2(F_2)[X]/⟨X³−1⟩`. Since
`X³ − 1 = (X+1)(X²+X+1)` is squarefree over `F_2`, CRT gives
`R_3 ≅ M_2(F_2) × M_2(F_4)`, so right ideals are pairs of right ideals and

$$\# = 5 \times 7 = 35,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb F_2} = 2v_1 + 4v_2,$$

with `v_1 ∈{0,1,2}` (multiplicities 1, 3, 1) and `v_2 ∈{0,1,2}` (multiplicities 1, 5, 1).
This predicts the dimension distribution
`[(0,1),(2,3),(4,6),(6,15),(8,6),(10,3),(12,1)]`, and the enumeration reproduces it
**exactly** (`u8_n3_selfdual_check.py`, `MATCH: True`; 4 096 ambient vectors scanned).
Two independent enumerations (lattice closure and Morita) agree on 35.

**(II) Quaternary convention.** A1's other result is a bijection between cyclic
`A`-codes of length `n` and cyclic codes over `F_4` of length `n`, via
`F_4 = ⟨I, ω⟩ ⊂ A`. The number of those is the number of monic divisors of `X^n − 1`
**over `F_4`**: at `n = 3`, `X³ − 1 = (X+1)(X+ω)(X+ω²)` gives `2³ = 8`
(verified: the 8 monic divisors were enumerated explicitly). **This is A1's "8".**
Dimensions over `F_2` are `4(3 − deg g) ∈ {0,4,8,12}`.

⇒ **The "8" is a quaternary-convention number; the "35" is a right-ideal-convention
number. They count different things, and neither is wrong.**
This is worth one explicit sentence in the manuscript: it is a *convention* divergence,
not a dispute.

## 3. Which convention carries the duality theorems — and where A1's theorem can live

The decisive test: **self-duality**.

| | Family (I): right ideals (35) | Family (II): quaternary (8) |
|---|---|---|
| possible `F_2`-dimensions | 0, 2, 4, **6**, 8, 10, 12 | 0, 4, 8, 12 |
| half-dimension (= 6) reachable? | **yes** (15 codes of dim 6) | **no** |
| self-orthogonal codes (form `Σ tr(x_j^T y_j)`) | 4 (incl. the zero code); 3 of dim 6 | 0 non-zero |
| **self-dual codes (form `Σ tr(x_j^T y_j)`)** | **3** (dim 6) | **0** |
| self-dual codes (form `Σ x_j y_j`, no transpose) | **0** | **0** |

Consequences:

1. Whatever A1's theorem says, it **cannot** be "the self-dual cyclic codes of length 3
   are generated by …" in the quaternary convention (no such code exists) and it does not
   match the right-ideal convention either (there the three self-dual codes are not
   principal generators of that shape — see `u8_n3_selfdual_log.txt` for their explicit
   bases).
2. The most plausible reading compatible with the recorded polynomials is
   **"self-orthogonal codes at `n = 7`"**: self-orthogonality needs
   `dim C = 4(7 − deg g) ≤ 14`, i.e. `deg g ≥ 4`, and the divisors of `X⁷ − 1` of degree
   ≥ 4 are `(X+1)(X³+X+1)`, `(X+1)(X³+X²+1)`, `(X³+X+1)(X³+X²+1)`, `X⁷+1` — the recorded
   list contains the last two. Even this is **not verified** and must not be asserted.
3. A1's **Euclidean form is `Σ_j x_j y_j` (matrix product, no transpose)**. That form is
   *not* non-degenerate in the `F_q`-bilinear sense: `dim C + dim C^⊥` is not constant
   (`[(6,15),(8,10),(10,6),(12,4)]` at `q=2,n=3`) and there are **0** self-dual codes.
   Our Galois form `B_s(x,y) = Σ θ_s(x_e)y_e` at `s = 0` is `Σ x_e^T y_e`, which *is*
   non-degenerate (`dim C + dim C^⊥ = 12` for all 35). **The manuscript must distinguish
   the two forms explicitly** (F-07); comparing our hulls with A1's without this remark
   would be a silent convention clash.

* **(b) Authorise the following position (recommended):**
  *cite A1 for the cyclic-code-over-`M_2(F_2)` framework, the residue/torsion description

Rationale for recommending (b): the audit can prove everything in (b) — it is all

available here. Standing rule: *never assert a published theorem is wrong.*

## 4. Coefficient centre

One further convention point, now settled: **our coefficient centre is `F_q`**
(`Z(A_t) = F_q[u]/⟨u^t⟩` if `u` is central; the *field* of coefficients for the
factorisation in T-018 is `F_q`, not `F_4`). A1's parametrization uses `F_4`-coefficients
(`ω ∈ M_2(F_2)`). Mixing them is what produced the earlier 27-vs-35 confusion. Our own
T-019 (centrality diagnosis) already records: a triple `(f,g,h)` with `f,g,h ∈ F_4[X]`
produces a right ideal under reading R1 **iff** `f,g,h ∈ F_2[X] = Z(A)[X]` — verified
exhaustively over all 27 triples. Keep `F_q` as the coefficient centre throughout
Phase 1; mention `F_4` only inside the (II)-convention paragraph.

# U9 — Is reflexivity genuinely necessary?

## 1. What is true (proved and verified)

Write `C^{⊥} := C^{⊥_s}`. Then, for every right submodule `C` and every `s`:

1. `|C|·|C^{⊥}| = |R|^n` (size formula) — **proved**, no hypothesis;
2. `(C^{⊥})^{⊥} = σ_{2s}(C)` — **proved**, no hypothesis; verified 6 961/6 961;
3. `σ_{2s}` is a semilinear isometry: `B_s(σ_{2s}x, σ_{2s}y) = σ_{2s}(B_s(x,y))`,
   it preserves Hamming weight, and it intertwines both the dual (E4) and the hull (E5);
4. hence `C^{⊥⊥}` has **the same cardinality, the same weight enumerator, the same
   minimum distance and the same hull dimension** as `C`.

Reflexivity (`C^{⊥⊥} = C` for all `C`) holds **iff** `r | 2s`, and only then
(35/737 at `q = 8` for both `s = 1, 2`, versus 343/343, 144/144, 5 000/5 000 elsewhere).

## 2. What reflexivity is **not** needed for

Every quantity that enters a hull-based construction is `σ_{2s}`-invariant, by §1.3–§1.4:

* self-orthogonality tests `C ⊆ C^{⊥}` (a *set* statement about `C`, not about `C^{⊥⊥}`);
* `dim Hull_s(C) = dim(C ∩ C^{⊥})` and hence the EAQEC/entanglement parameter
  `c = dim C − dim Hull_s(C)`;
* the size/`k`-bookkeeping `|C|·|C^{⊥}| = |R|^n`;
* the weight enumerator and the minimum distance of `C` and of `Hull_s(C)`.

So: **if** the quantum parameter formula is derived by *counting* (dimension of the
stabiliser group, rank of the commutation/symplectic matrix, coset counting), reflexivity
is not needed and the theorem holds for every `s`.

## 3. What reflexivity **is** needed for

* Any proof that goes through `C^{⊥⊥}` literally: the classic CSS chain
  `C_2 ⊆ C_1^{⊥}` ⇒ `k = dim C_1 − dim C_2^{⊥}`-type arguments, LCD
  (`C ⊕ C^{⊥} = V`), and "the dual of the dual is the code" steps in MacWilliams
  derivations;
* the statement that `⊥` is an involution of the code lattice (needed if one wants
  `C ↦ C^{⊥}` to be a Galois connection, or wants to enumerate "dual pairs" cleanly);
* the *symmetry* of the form: by the key identity
  `θ_s(B_s(x,y)) = B_s(y, σ_{2s}x)`, the form is symmetric **iff** `σ_{2s} = id`.
  In the non-reflexive regime, checking self-orthogonality on pairs `(g_i, g_j)` with
  `i ≤ j` (the usual `G G^† = 0` shortcut) is **not valid** — one must check all ordered
  pairs (F-06). This is a silent-trap risk for Phase 4–6.

## 4. Comparison of the two parameter regimes

| | `q = p^{2e}`, `s = r/2` ("Galois–Hermitian") | `s ≡ 0` (Euclidean) | general `q = p^r`, `r ≥ 2`, `s` arbitrary |
|---|---|---|---|
| `σ_s` | non-trivial, order **2** | trivial | order `r/gcd(r,s)` |
| `r | 2s`? | yes (`2s = r`) | yes | **only if `r | 2s`** |
| `θ_s` | **involution** (`θ_s² = id`) | involution (transpose) | `θ_s² = σ_{2s} ≠ id` in general |
| reflexive? | **yes** | yes | **no** in general (35/737 at q=8) |
| non-trivial Galois action? | **yes** | no | yes |
| odd `r` (e.g. `q = 8`, `r = 3`)? | impossible | only trivial option | non-trivial ⇒ never reflexive |

**Structural remark.** For `r` odd, `r | 2s ⟺ r | s ⟺ σ_s = id`, so **non-triviality of
the Galois action and reflexivity are mutually exclusive**. For `r` even, `s = r/2` is
the unique non-trivial reflexive choice (up to `s ≡ r/2 mod r`). This is the cleanest
statement of the constraint and is the one the manuscript should use.

**Note on `q = p` (r = 1).** Then `σ_s = id` for all `s`, so there is **no** Galois
action at all: the whole Galois-hull programme is vacuous over prime fields. Phase 1 must
require `r ≥ 2` explicitly (and `r` even for the reflexive non-trivial case).

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Main quantum theorems (Phase 6) in the reflexive regime**: either `s ≡ 0`
   (Euclidean; always available, useful as a baseline) or `r` even with `s = r/2`
   (the genuine Galois–Hermitian case, and the directly novel one). State the hypothesis
   `r | 2s` in the theorem, not in a remark.
2. **Companion theorems for general `s`**: keep `(C^{⊥})^{⊥} = σ_{2s}(C)`, the size
   formula, hull closure and the isometry lemma E1–E5 for **all** `s`, with no reflexivity
   hypothesis. Present the non-reflexive regime as "all numerical invariants are
   unchanged, but `⊥` is not an involution".
3. **Do not** claim a CSS/EA parameter formula in the non-reflexive regime unless it is
   derived by direct counting (no `C^{⊥⊥}` step) — and if it is, say so explicitly in the
   proof.
4. **Do not** use the `G θ_s(G)^T = 0` self-orthogonality shortcut unless `r | 2s`
   (F-06).
5. Flag for later (F-13): check whether the MacWilliams identities of Szabo–Wood (2017)
   require an involutive/hermitian setting before claiming them in the non-reflexive
   regime.

**This settles U9: reflexivity is not needed for the hull/size/distance data, but it is
needed for the classical double-duality proofs and for the symmetry of the form; adopt the
reflexive regime for the quantum theorems and keep the general-`s` statements as
companions.**
