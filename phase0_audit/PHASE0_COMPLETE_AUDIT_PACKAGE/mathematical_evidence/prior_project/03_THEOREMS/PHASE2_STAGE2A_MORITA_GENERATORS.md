## 1. Standing hypotheses and notation

Throughout:

| symbol | meaning |
|---|---|
| `q = p^r`, `t ≥ 1`, `n ≥ 1` | the parameters |
| `A_t = F_q[u]/⟨u^t⟩` | commutative finite chain ring, `|A_t| = q^t` |
| `R = M_2(A_t)` | the matrix ring |
| `B = A_t[X]/⟨X^n−1⟩` | `X` **central** ⇒ `B` is a **commutative** finite ring, `|B| = q^{tn}`, `dim_{F_q} B = tn` |
| `R_n = R[X]/⟨X^n−1⟩` | the cyclic ambient of Stage 1 |
| `S = M_2(B)` | `≅ End_B(P_B)`, acting on columns by left multiplication |
| `P = B^2` | the right `B`-module of **columns** `v = (v_1, v_2)^T` |

For a `B`-submodule `W ⊆ P`:

* `I_W := {M ∈ S : im M ⊆ W} = {M ∈ S : col_1(M), col_2(M) ∈ W}`;
* for a right ideal `I ⊆ S`: `W(I) := Σ_{M ∈ I} im M ⊆ P`.

For a module `M` over a ring `Λ`:

* `μ_Λ(M) := min{k ≥ 0 : M is generated by k elements}`; `μ_Λ(0) := 0`;
* "principal" for a right ideal of `S` means `μ_S(I) ≤ 1`, i.e. `I = xS` for some `x ∈ S`.

Conventions that are used without further comment (all fixed in Stage 1, §3):

1. `S` acts on `P` by left multiplication on columns, and the map `M ↦ (v ↦ Mv)` is a
   ring **isomorphism** `S ≅ End_B(P_B)` — with the usual matrix product, because
   `(MN)v = M(Nv)`, i.e. `f_{MN} = f_M ∘ f_N` (Lemma 1.1).
2. A right ideal of `S` is closed under `M ↦ MN` (`N` acts first).
3. `im M := M(P) = {Mv : v ∈ P}` is the `B`-submodule of `P` generated by the two columns
   of `M` (Lemma 1.2).

### Lemma 1.1 (`S = End_B(P_B)`) — **PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A**

*Proof.* `f_M : v ↦ Mv` is `B`-linear: `f_M(vb) = M(vb) = (Mv)b` (matrix multiplication is
`B`-bilinear). `M ↦ f_M` is additive and injective (`f_M(e_j) = col_j(M)`). It is
surjective: for `f ∈ End_B(P_B)`, `f` is determined by `f(e_1), f(e_2)`, and
`f = f_M` for `M = [f(e_1) | f(e_2)]`. Finally it is multiplicative in the sense of the
composition product on `End_B(P_B)`: `f_{MN}(v) = M(Nv) = f_M(f_N(v)) = (f_M ∘ f_N)(v)`. ∎

### Lemma 1.2 (image of a matrix) — **PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A**

For `M ∈ S` with columns `c_1, c_2 ∈ P`: `im M = c_1B + c_2B`.

*Proof.* `Mv = c_1v_1 + c_2v_2` for `v = (v_1,v_2)^T`, so `im M ⊆ c_1B + c_2B`; and
`c_j = M e_j ∈ im M`. (No commutativity of `B` is used: the coefficients `v_j` act on the
right, which is the side the module structure provides.) ∎

### Lemma 1.3 (the identification `R_n = M_2(B)`) — **PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A**

The map
`Φ : R_n = M_2(A_t)[X]/⟨X^n−1⟩ → M_2(B)`, `Φ(Σ_k M_k X^k) := ( Σ_k (M_k)_{ij} X^k )_{i,j}`
is a ring isomorphism, and it coincides with the Stage-1 coefficient representation `ι`
in the sense that `Φ(ι(x_0,…,x_{n−1})) = ( Σ_k (x_k)_{ij} X^k )_{i,j}`.

*Proof.* Both sides are free `A_t`-modules with `A_t`-bases `{E_{ij}X^k}` and
`{E_{ij} X^k}` respectively, and `Φ` is `A_t`-linear and maps basis to basis, hence is an
`A_t`-linear bijection. For multiplicativity let `x = Σ_k M_kX^k`, `y = Σ_l N_lX^l`; then
`xy = Σ_e (Σ_{k+l≡e mod n} M_kN_l) X^e`, so
`Φ(xy)_{ij} = Σ_e Σ_{k+l≡e} Σ_m (M_k)_{im}(N_l)_{mj} X^{k+l}
= Σ_m (Σ_k (M_k)_{im}X^k)(Σ_l (N_l)_{mj}X^l) = (Φ(x)Φ(y))_{ij}`,
using `X^{k+l} = X^{k+l mod n}` in `B` and that `A_t` lies in the centre of `B` (`X` is
central). ∎

**Remark.** Lemma 1.3 is what makes the two correspondences comparable: Stage 1 attaches
to a right ideal `I ⊆ R_n` the `T`-invariant right `R`-submodule `ι^{−1}(I) ⊆ R^n`
(Stage 1, Theorem 4.1), while §2 attaches to `I ⊆ S = M_2(B)` the `B`-submodule
`W(I) ⊆ B^2`. Theorem 2.9 says these are the same correspondence.

## 2. Task A — the Morita correspondence

### 2.1 The statement

**Theorem 2.1 (Morita correspondence; = P-2.3(1)) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.**

Let `S = M_2(B)`, `P = B^2` (columns). Then:

1. for every `B`-submodule `W ⊆ P`, `I_W` is a right ideal of `S`, and
   `I_W = Hom_B(P,W)` inside `S = End_B(P)`;
2. for every right ideal `I ⊆ S`, `W(I)` is a `B`-submodule of `P`;
3. `W(I_W) = W` for every `W`, and `I_{W(I)} = I` for every right ideal `I`; hence
   `W ↦ I_W` is a **bijection** from the `B`-submodules of `P` onto the right ideals of
   `S`, with inverse `I ↦ W(I)`;
4. the bijection preserves and reflects inclusion and preserves sums and intersections:
   `W_1 ⊆ W_2 ⟺ I_{W_1} ⊆ I_{W_2}`, `I_{W_1+W_2} = I_{W_1}+I_{W_2}`,
   `I_{W_1∩W_2} = I_{W_1}∩I_{W_2}`;
5. as right `B`-modules `I_W ≅ W ⊕ W` (via the columns), so
   `dim_{F_q} I_W = 2 dim_{F_q} W` and `|I_W| = |W|^2`.

**Hypotheses.** None beyond `B` unital; `B` need not be commutative, finite or local.
(Commutativity is used only from §2.6 on, for the transpose dictionary.)

### 2.2 Proofs

**Lemma 2.2 (= L-2.3.1, first part) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.**
`I_W` is a right ideal of `S`, and `I_W = Hom_B(P,W)`.

*Proof.* If `M ∈ I_W` and `N ∈ S` then `im(MN) ⊆ im M ⊆ W`, so `MN ∈ I_W`; closure under
addition is clear because `im(M+M′) ⊆ im M + im M′ ⊆ W`. For the second claim:
`M ∈ I_W` iff every `Mv ∈ W`, i.e. iff the `B`-linear map `f_M : P → P` has image in `W`,
i.e. `f_M ∈ Hom_B(P,W)`. ∎

**Lemma 2.3 (= L-2.3.1, second part) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.** `W(I_W) = W`.

*Proof.* `⊆`: every `M ∈ I_W` has `im M ⊆ W`, and `W` is closed under sums. `⊇`: let
`w ∈ W`; the matrix `M_w := [w | 0]` (first column `w`, second column `0`) lies in `I_W`
by Lemma 1.2, and `w = M_w e_1 ∈ im M_w ⊆ W(I_W)`. ∎

**Lemma 2.4 (column packing) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.**
Let `M_1,…,M_k ∈ S` and let `Z ∈ S` have columns `z_1, z_2 ∈ Σ_i im M_i`. Then there are
`N_1,…,N_k ∈ S` with `Z = Σ_i M_iN_i`.

*Proof.* Write `z_j = Σ_i M_iv_{ij}` with `v_{ij} ∈ P` (this is what membership in the sum
of the images means). Put `N_i := [v_{i1} | v_{i2}] ∈ M_2(B) = S`. Then the first column of
`Σ_i M_iN_i` is `Σ_i M_i v_{i1} = z_1` and its second column is `Σ_i M_i v_{i2} = z_2`, so
`Σ_i M_iN_i = Z`. ∎

**Lemma 2.5 (surjectivity: `I_{W(I)} = I`) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.**

*Proof.* `⊇`: if `M ∈ I` then `im M ⊆ W(I)`, so `M ∈ I_{W(I)}`. `⊆`: let
`M ∈ I_{W(I)}`, i.e. `im M ⊆ W(I) = Σ_{M′∈I} im M′`. Each column of `M` lies in that sum,
hence in a **finite** sub-sum `Σ_{i=1}^k im M_i` with `M_i ∈ I` (take the union of the two
finite index sets, padding with zero summands). By Lemma 2.4, `M = Σ_i M_iN_i` with
`N_i ∈ S`, and since `I` is a right ideal, `M ∈ I`. ∎

**Proof of Theorem 2.1.** (1) Lemma 2.2. (2) `W(I)` is a sum of submodules `im M`, hence a
submodule. (3) Lemma 2.3 and Lemma 2.5 — the two composites are the identity on both
sides, so `W ↦ I_W` is a bijection with inverse `I ↦ W(I)`. (4) Inclusion is immediate
from the definition of `I_W` in both directions. Sums: `I_{W_1}+I_{W_2} ⊆ I_{W_1+W_2}` is
clear; conversely, if `Z ∈ I_{W_1+W_2}` then its columns `z_j = w_{1j}+w_{2j}` with
`w_{ij} ∈ W_i`, and `Z = [w_{11}|w_{12}] + [w_{21}|w_{22}] ∈ I_{W_1}+I_{W_2}`.
Intersections: `im M ⊆ W_1 ∩ W_2 ⟺ (im M ⊆ W_1 and im M ⊆ W_2)`. (5) The map
`M ↦ (col_1 M, col_2 M)` is an isomorphism of right `B`-modules `I_W → W ⊕ W` (right
scalar multiplication acts columnwise); the dimension and cardinality statements follow. ∎

**Remark 2.6.** The whole of §2.2 is elementary: no projectivity, no Morita theorem, no
finiteness, no commutativity. This matters for §3, where the *same* packing argument
proves L-2.3a without any projectivity hypothesis.

### 2.3 Left ideals

Let `τ : S → S`, `τ(M) = M^T`, be the transpose.

**Proposition 2.7 (left ideals) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.**
Assume `B` commutative. Then `τ` is a ring anti-automorphism of `S` (`(MN)^T = N^TM^T`),
so `I ↦ I^T` is an inclusion-preserving bijection from the right ideals of `S` onto the
left ideals of `S`, with inverse the transpose again. Consequently the left ideals of `S`
are exactly the `L_V := {M ∈ S : row_1(M), row_2(M) ∈ V}` for `V` running over the
`B`-submodules of `P` (rows), and `#{left ideals} = #{right ideals}`.

*Proof.* With `B` commutative, `(MN)^T = N^TM^T` holds entrywise, so `τ(MN) = τ(N)τ(M)`;
`τ^2 = id`. If `I` is a right ideal and `N ∈ S`, `M ∈ I`, then `N τ(M) = τ(τ(N)M) ∈ τ(I)`
because `τ(N)M ∈ I`; so `τ(I)` is a left ideal. The rest is `τ^2 = id` plus Theorem 2.1
applied to the transposed picture (or directly: `M ∈ L_V ⟺ M^T ∈ I_V`). ∎

**Warning 2.8 (the transpose does not commute with the correspondence).** For a submodule
`V ⊆ P`, `L_V = (I_V)^T` — the left ideal attached to `V` is the transpose of the right
ideal attached to `V`, and in general `L_V ≠ I_V`. Both dictionaries use the same indexing
set of submodules, but they are different maps. (Verified computationally: §5.)

### 2.4 Two-sided ideals

**Proposition 2.9 (two-sided ideals; = P-2.3(3)) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A**
(classical source: E-003).

Assume `B` commutative. The two-sided ideals of `S = M_2(B)` are exactly the
`M_2(J) = {M ∈ S : every entry of M lies in J}` for `J` an ideal of `B`; the correspondence
`J ↦ M_2(J)` is an inclusion-preserving bijection from the ideals of `B` onto the
two-sided ideals of `S`. Under Theorem 2.1, the two-sided ideal `M_2(J)` corresponds to
the submodule `W = J ⊕ J = JB^2 ⊆ P`.

*Proof.* Let `I` be a two-sided ideal and put
`J := {a ∈ B : aE_{11} ∈ I}` (an ideal of `B`: if `aE_{11}, a′E_{11} ∈ I` then
`(a+a′)E_{11} ∈ I`, and for `b ∈ B`, `baE_{11} = (bI_2)(aE_{11}) ∈ I` and
`abE_{11} = (aE_{11})(bI_2) ∈ I`). For `M = (m_{ij}) ∈ I` we get
`m_{ij}E_{11} = E_{1i}ME_{j1} ∈ I`, so every entry of every `M ∈ I` lies in `J`, i.e.
`I ⊆ M_2(J)`. Conversely if `a ∈ J` then `aE_{11} ∈ I`, hence
`aE_{ij} = E_{i1}(aE_{11})E_{1j} ∈ I` for all `i,j`, so `M_2(J) ⊆ I` (every element of
`M_2(J)` is a sum of such). The last sentence: `M ∈ I_{J⊕J} ⟺` both columns of `M` lie in
`JB^2 ⟺` all entries of `M` lie in `J`. ∎

### 2.5 The dictionary with the Stage-1 cyclic-code representation

Stage 1 (Theorem 4.1) gives a bijection

`{right ideals of R_n} ↔ {T-invariant right R-submodules of R^n}`, `I ↦ ι^{−1}(I)`,

where `ι(x_0,…,x_{n−1}) = Σ_e x_eX^e` and `T` is the cyclic shift. Transporting `T`
through the Morita equivalence for `R = M_2(A_t)` turns `T`-invariance into
`B`-linearity. Concretely, define the **regrouping map**

`ρ : (A_t^2)^n → (A_t^n)^2 = B^2`,
`ρ((w_0^1,w_0^2),…,(w_{n−1}^1,w_{n−1}^2)) := ( Σ_k w_k^1 X^k , Σ_k w_k^2 X^k )`,

an `A_t`-linear isomorphism, and for a right `R`-submodule `C ⊆ R^n` put

`C·P_A := A_t-span{ (M_0v, …, M_{n−1}v) : (M_0,…,M_{n−1}) ∈ C, v ∈ A_t^2 } ⊆ (A_t^2)^n`.

**Theorem 2.10 (the two correspondences agree) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.**
For every right ideal `I ⊆ R_n ≅ S` (Lemma 1.3), with `C := ι^{−1}(I)`:

`W(I) = ρ( C·P_A ) ⊆ B^2`.

*Proof.* For `(M_0,…,M_{n−1}) ∈ C` and `v = (v_1,v_2)^T ∈ A_t^2`,
`(M_kv)^{(i)} = Σ_j (M_k)_{ij}v_j`, hence
`ρ((M_0v,…,M_{n−1}v)) = ( Σ_j (Σ_k (M_k)_{1j}X^k) v_j , Σ_j (Σ_k (M_k)_{2j}X^k) v_j )
= Φ(ι(M)) · v̄`, where `v̄ = (v_1,v_2)^T ∈ B^2` and the product is the column action of
`Φ(ι(M)) ∈ M_2(B)`. As `M` runs over `C` and `v` over `A_t^2`, `Φ(ι(M))` runs over `I`
(`ι : C → I` is a bijection) and `v̄` runs over the `A_t`-submodule `A_t^2 ⊆ B^2`. Therefore
`ρ(C·P_A) = A_t-span{ N w : N ∈ I, w ∈ A_t^2 }`, which is `A_t`-spanned by the columns of
the elements of `I`. This contains `Σ_{N∈I} im N = W(I)` (each `im N` is `B`-spanned by
the columns, and `A_t ⊆ B`), and it is contained in `W(I)` because `W(I)` is a
`B`-submodule containing every column of every `N ∈ I`. ∎

**Remark 2.11.** Theorem 2.10 is the precise sense in which the Stage-1 "cyclic code =
right ideal of `R_n`" dictionary and the classical Morita dictionary are one and the same
correspondence: a `T`-invariant right `R`-submodule of `R^n` is exactly a `B`-submodule of
`B^2`, transported by `ρ`. In particular the number of cyclic codes of length `n` over
`M_2(A_t)` **is** the number of `B`-submodules of `B^2` — which is why the counts of §5.2
reproduce the Phase-1 counts (7 at `(4,1,1)`, 12 at `(9,1,1)`, 11 at `(8,1,1)`, 33 at
`(4,1,2)`, 33 at `(4,2,1)`, 343 at `(4,1,3)`, 35 at `(2,1,3)`).

## 3. Task B — the generator-number identity

**Theorem 3.1 (= P-2.3(2) = P-2.5(a)) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.**
For every `B`-submodule `W ⊆ P = B^2`:

`μ_S(I_W) = ⌈ μ_B(W) / 2 ⌉`.

**Hypotheses.** `B` a unital ring (no commutativity, finiteness or projectivity needed),
`S = M_2(B)`, `P = B^2` with the conventions of §1. The identity therefore holds for every
`B = A_t[X]/⟨X^n−1⟩`, in particular in the wild case.

The proof occupies §3.1–§3.4; §3.5 gives an independent second derivation (Nakayama on the
semisimple quotient) which explains the `2` and the `⌈⌉`; §3.6 states precisely what
projectivity does and does not do.

### 3.1 L-2.3a (set equality)

**Lemma 3.2 (= L-2.3a) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A (two proofs).**
For `x_1,…,x_k ∈ S`, with `W′ := Σ_i im x_i`:

`Σ_{i=1}^k x_iS = I_{W′}`.

*Proof 1 (column packing; uses nothing).* `⊆`: `im(x_iN_i) ⊆ im x_i ⊆ W′` and `I_{W′}` is
closed under addition. `⊇`: this is Lemma 2.4 verbatim (with `M_i = x_i`, `Z ∈ I_{W′}`). ∎

*Proof 2 (lifting; uses projectivity — this is the route of `PHASE2_PROPOSAL_v2.md` §3).*
`⊆` as above. For `⊇` let `g ∈ I_{W′}`, i.e. `g : P → P` is `B`-linear with
`im g ⊆ W′`. The map `Θ : P^k → W′`, `(v_1,…,v_k) ↦ Σ_i x_iv_i`, is `B`-linear and
surjective by the definition of `W′`. Since `P = B^2` is **free, hence projective**, `g`
lifts along the surjection `Θ`: there is a `B`-linear `G : P → P^k` with `Θ ∘ G = g`.
Writing `G = (M_1,…,M_k)` with `M_i ∈ End_B(P) = S` (Lemma 1.1) gives
`g = Σ_i x_iM_i ∈ Σ_i x_iS`. ∎

**Where projectivity is used.** Only in Proof 2, only at the single step "surjective `Θ`
⇒ every `g : P → W′` lifts". Proof 1 has no such step: it replaces the lift by the
explicit matrices `N_i = [v_{i1}|v_{i2}]`, which exist because `S = M_2(B)` is the **full**
`2×2` matrix ring and `P` has exactly two coordinate slots to fill. Consequently:

* **the conclusion of L-2.3a does not need projectivity** (it holds for every unital `B`);
* the *scheme* of Proof 2 does — see Proposition 3.3, which shows that for a general
  `B`-module `P` and `S = End_B(P)` the statement is false without projectivity.

### 3.2 Projectivity is not removable in the general form of L-2.3a

**Proposition 3.3 (counterexample) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.**
Let `B = F_2[ε]/⟨ε^2⟩` (this is `A_t[X]/⟨X^n−1⟩` at `(q,t,n) = (2,1,2)`), let
`k = B/(ε) ≅ F_2` and let `P′ := B ⊕ k` as a right `B`-module (the `B`-action on `k`
factors through `B → k`). Put `S′ := End_B(P′)` (`B` acts on `P′` on the right, so `S′`
acts on the left and `S′`-multiplication is composition). Then:

1. `P′` is **not projective**;
2. with `x ∈ S′` defined by `x(b,m) = (εb, 0)`, one has `xS′ ⊊ I_{im x}`, i.e. **L-2.3a
   fails for `P′`**.

*Proof.* (1) If `P′` were projective then its direct summand `k` would be projective, so
the surjection `π : B → k` would admit a `B`-linear section `σ`. Such a `σ` is determined
by `b_0 := σ(1)`; `B`-linearity forces `εb_0 = σ(ε·1) = σ(0) = 0`, so `b_0 ∈ ann(ε) = εB`,
whence `π(b_0) = 0 ≠ 1`. Contradiction. (Computationally: the only elements with
`εb_0 = 0` are `0, ε`, both mapping to `0`.)

(2) Let `im x = {(εb,0)} = {(0,0), (ε,0)}` (as `εB = {0,ε}`). Take
`g(b,m) := (εm̄, 0)` where `m̄ ∈ B` is the constant lift of `m ∈ k`. Then `g` is
`B`-linear: for `a ∈ B`, `g((b,m)a) = (ε·\overline{ma},0) = (ε m̄ ā, 0)`, while
`g(b,m)a = (ε m̄ a, 0) = (ε m̄ ā + ε m̄ ãε, 0) = (ε m̄ ā, 0)` because `ε^2 = 0`. And
`im g = im x`, so `g ∈ I_{im x}`. But `g ∉ xS′`: `g = x ∘ h` would force, at `v = (0,1)`,
`ε h(v)_1 = ε`, i.e. `h(0,1)_1 ∈ 1 + εB`; whereas `B`-linearity of `h` forces
`ε · h(0,1) = h((0,1)ε) = h(0,0) = 0`, i.e. `h(0,1)_1 ∈ εB = {0,ε}`. These are
disjoint, so no such `h` exists. (Computationally: `|S′| = 32`, `|xS′| = 2`,
`|I_{im x}| = 4`.) ∎

**Remark 3.4.** Proposition 3.3 is a counterexample to the *generalisation* of L-2.3a to
arbitrary modules `P`, **not** to L-2.3a itself, which is proved for `P = B^2` in Lemma
3.2 without any hypothesis. The register row records the projectivity hypothesis of the
lifting route as removable for `P = B^2` and non-removable in general.

### 3.3 L-2.3b (counting) and L-2.3c (realisability)

**Lemma 3.5 (= L-2.3b) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.**

`min{ k : W = Σ_{i=1}^k U_i with μ_B(U_i) ≤ 2 for all i } = ⌈ μ_B(W)/2 ⌉`.

*Proof.* Let `m := μ_B(W)`. `(≥)` If `W = Σ_i U_i` with `U_i` generated by `u_i ≤ 2`

*Proof.* Lemma 1.2. ∎

### 3.5 An independent derivation (Nakayama), and where the `2` comes from

This section is a **cross-check**, not part of the proof of Theorem 3.1; it uses standard
structure theory and explains the shape of the formula.

Let `B` be our finite commutative ring, `J := Jac(B) =` its nilradical (`B` is commutative
artinian), `K := B/J` (a product of finite fields), `J(S) = M_2(J) = Jac(S)` and
`S/J(S) ≅ M_2(K)`. For a finitely generated right `S`-module `M`,

`μ_S(M) = μ_{S/J(S)}(M/MJ(S))` (Nakayama, both directions: generators lift, and
`M = Σm_iS + MJ(S)` with `M/Σm_iS` finitely generated forces `M = Σm_iS`).

Over `M_2(K)` with `K = ∏_i K_i`, every right module is `⊕_i V_i` with `V_i` a right
`M_2(K_i)`-module; `M_2(K_i)` is simple artinian with unique simple right module `K_i^2`
(rows), and `M_2(K_i) ≅ (K_i^2) ⊕ (K_i^2)` as a right module over itself. Hence
`V_i ≅ (K_i^2)^{d_i}` and `μ_{M_2(K_i)}(V_i) = ⌈d_i/2⌉`, so
`μ_S(M) = max_i ⌈d_i/2⌉ = ⌈ max_i d_i / 2 ⌉`.

Applying this to `I = I_W`: over `K_i`, `I/JI` corresponds (Morita over the field `K_i`)
to `W_i := (W/JW)_i ⊆ K_i^2`, and `d_i = dim_{K_i} W_i`; while
`μ_B(W) = μ_K(W/JW) = max_i dim_{K_i} W_i`. Hence `μ_S(I_W) = ⌈μ_B(W)/2⌉` again. The `2`
is the length of `M_2(K)` as a module over itself in simples: **one generator of a right
ideal covers at most two `K_i`-dimensions' worth of simple module**, which is exactly what
"packing two generators into the two columns of one matrix" does in the elementary proof.

The computation of §5 computes `μ` by this route as well (brute force on the small
semisimple quotient) and cross-checks it against two other methods.

### 3.6 Summary: what projectivity does here

| statement | projectivity needed? |
|---|---|
| Theorem 2.1 (the lattice bijection) | **no** (Lemma 2.4 does the work) |
| L-2.3a for `P = B^2` | **no** (packing), though the standard route uses freeness of `B^2` |
| L-2.3a for a general `B`-module `P`, `S = End_B(P)` | **yes** — false without it (Prop. 3.3) |

So the identity of Theorem 3.1 is not a consequence of projectivity: the register entry
for P-2.3(2) may be upgraded on the strength of §3.1–§3.4 alone, with §3.5 as an
independent confirmation and Proposition 3.3 as the delimitation of the standard route.

## 4. Task C — principality at the level of an individual ideal

### 4.1 The criterion

**Theorem 4.1 (= P-2.5(b)) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.**
For every `B`-submodule `W ⊆ P`: `I_W` is principal `⟺ μ_B(W) ≤ 2`.

*Proof.* `I_W = MS` for some `M ∈ S` `⟺ I_W = I_{im M}` (Lemma 3.2 with `k = 1`)
`⟺ W = im M` (Theorem 2.1(3)) `⟺ W` is generated by the two columns of `M`
(Lemma 1.2) `⟺ μ_B(W) ≤ 2` (if `μ_B(W) ≤ 2`, take `M = [w_1|w_2]` for a generating
pair, padding with `0`). ∎

**Corollary 4.2 (= P-2.5(c)) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.**
`μ_B(W) ≤ 4 ⇒ μ_S(I_W) ≤ 2` (i.e. `I_W` is `2`-generated). *Proof.* Theorem 3.1. ∎

**Corollary 4.3 — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.** A submodule `W ⊆ B^2` is of the form
`im M` for some `M ∈ M_2(B)` iff it is `≤ 2`-generated. ∎

### 4.2 An explicit non-principal right ideal in the wild case

**Theorem 4.4 (wild counterexample, individual) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.**
Let `(q,t,n) = (4,2,2)`, so `B = A_2[X]/⟨X^2−1⟩ = F_4[u,ε]/⟨u^2,ε^2⟩` with `ε := X+1`
(char `2`: `X^2−1 = (X+1)^2`). Then `B` is **local** with maximal ideal
`m = (u,ε) = F_4u ⊕ F_4ε ⊕ F_4uε`, `B/m ≅ F_4`, `m^2 = F_4uε`, `dim_{F_4}(m/m^2) = 2`.
For `W := m ⊕ m ⊆ B^2`:

1. `μ_B(W) = 4`;
2. `I_W = M_2(m)` and `μ_S(I_W) = 2`, so `I_W` is **not principal**.

*Proof.* (1) `W` is generated over `B` by `ue_1, εe_1, ue_2, εe_2`, so `μ_B(W) ≤ 4`.
Conversely, if `w_1,…,w_k` generate `W` over `B`, their images generate the
`B/m`-vector space `W/mW` (the quotient map is `B`-linear and onto), and
`W/mW = (m/m^2) ⊕ (m/m^2)` has `F_4`-dimension `2+2 = 4`; hence `k ≥ 4`.

(2) `I_W = {M : both columns in m⊕m} = M_2(m)`. Upper bound: `I_W` is generated by
`M_1 := [ue_1 | εe_1]` and `M_2 := [ue_2 | εe_2]`: by Lemma 3.2,
`M_1S + M_2S = I_{im M_1 + im M_2} = I_{m e_1 + m e_2} = I_W`. So `μ_S(I_W) ≤ 2`.
Lower bound: `J(S) = M_2(m)`, so `I_W J(S) = M_2(m)M_2(m) = M_2(m^2)` and
`I_W/I_WJ(S) ≅ M_2(m/m^2)`, which has `F_4`-dimension `4·2 = 8`. A principal right ideal
`xS` has `(xS + I_WJ(S))/I_WJ(S)` generated over `S/J(S) = M_2(F_4)` by the single image
of `x`, so its `F_4`-dimension is at most `dim_{F_4} M_2(F_4) = 4 < 8`. Hence `I_W` is not
principal, i.e. `μ_S(I_W) ≥ 2`. Therefore `μ_S(I_W) = 2 = ⌈4/2⌉`, in agreement with
Theorem 3.1. ∎

The same phenomenon is present, and **exhaustively verified**, at `(q,t,n) = (2,2,2)`
(where `B = F_2[u,ε]/⟨u^2,ε^2⟩`, `|B| = 16`): there the census finds exactly one submodule
with `μ_B = 4` (namely `m ⊕ m`) and 21 with `μ_B = 3`, and exactly 22 non-principal right
ideals, all with `μ_S = 2` (§5.2).

### 4.3 What is *not* claimed

**Corollary 4.5 (reduction of the global question) — PROVED IN PHASE 2 STAGE 2A.**
Every right ideal of `S = M_2(B)` is principal `⟺` every `B`-submodule of `B^2` is
generated by `≤ 2` elements.

*Proof.* Theorem 2.1(3) (every right ideal is `I_W` for a unique `W`) + Theorem 4.1. ∎

### 7.2 Refuted or modified

* **No claim of the proposal was refuted.** P-2.3(2) and P-2.5(a),(b),(c) are all
  **true as stated**, and are now proved.
* **Modified (strengthened):** the hypothesis of L-2.3a. `PHASE2_PROPOSAL_v2.md` proves
  L-2.3a by lifting and records "uses projectivity"; Stage 2A proves it with **no
  hypothesis at all** (column packing, Lemma 2.4) and delimits the standard route by
  Proposition 3.3: for a general `B`-module `P` with `S = End_B(P)` the statement is
  **false** without projectivity. So projectivity is a hypothesis of the *route*, not of
  the *result*, for `P = B^2`.
* **P-2.5-W / O-2.2** ("`μ_B(W) ≤ 4` for every `W`") is not claimed; Stage 2A observed
  `max μ_B = 4` at every enumerated parameter set, including the two wild rings, and

### 7.5 Remaining proof obligations and limitations

1. **P-2.6 (global principality) is untouched.** Corollary 4.5 reduces it to a statement
   about submodules of `B^2`, but that statement is proved here **only** in the individual
   cases displayed; the classification "exactly when `p ∤ n` or `t = 1`" needs P-2.4 and
   L-2.6.1–L-2.6.8, all still `PROPOSED / UNPROVED`.
2. **T-021 is not discharged**: Stage 2A proves that *some* right ideals at `(4,2,2)` and
   `(2,2,2)` are not principal; it does not decide principality in general for `t ≥ 2` or
   `p ∣ n`.
3. **No CRT/Hensel**: statements are proved without decomposing `B`; in particular no use
   is made of `B ≅ ∏ B_j`, and the componentwise formula `μ_B(W) = max_j μ_{B_j}(W_j)`
   (L-2.4.5 / L-2.6.5) is neither used nor checked.
4. **Coverage**: exhaustive only for `|B| ≤ 16` (`|S| ≤ 65536`); the rings of primary
   interest with `t ≥ 2, n ≥ 2` (`(4,2,2)`, `(2,2,3)`, …) are out of reach of exhaustive
   enumeration and were sampled/targeted only.
5. **O-2.2** (is `μ_B(W) ≤ 4` for every submodule of `B^2`?) remains open; the maximum
   observed is `4`.
6. Carried forward from Stage 1 and Phase 1 (unchanged): `rev` (P-2.1f, T-066),
   **L-6.1** (T-067), T-006, T-027, T-011–T-014, warning B3.5, the Phase-1 coverage gaps,
   and the `n ≥ 2` left-submodule gap.
7. The Nakayama cross-check (§3.5) uses standard structure theory (`Jac(B) = nilradical`
