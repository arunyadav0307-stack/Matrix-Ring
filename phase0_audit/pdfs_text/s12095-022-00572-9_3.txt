Cyclic codes over M4(𝔽2 + u𝔽2)  
Shikha Patel1 · Om Prakash1  · Habibul Islam1
© The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer Science+Business Media, LLC, part of Springer Nature 2022
Abstract
Let p be a prime and 𝔽q be a finite field for q = pm . In this paper, we consider the ring 
R = M4(𝔽2 + u𝔽2) of 4 × 4 matrices over the finite ring 𝔽2 + u𝔽2 with u2 = 0 . Then 
R is a noncommutative non-chain ring of cardinality 416 and isomorphic to the ring 
𝔽16 + v𝔽16 + v2𝔽16 + v3𝔽16 + u𝔽16 + uv𝔽16 + uv2𝔽16 + uv3𝔽16, where v4 = 0 , uv = vu , 
uv2 = v2u and uv3 = v3u . Here, first we establish the structure of cyclic codes and their 
generators over R and later the dual (Euclidean and Hermitian both) of these cyclic codes 
are discussed. Further, with the help of the Gray map, we show that the image of a cyclic 
code is an 𝔽16-linear code. Finally, we provide some non-trivial examples of linear codes 
with good parameters to support our derived results.
Keywords Modules · Matrix ring · Cyclic codes · Gray map

1 Introduction
The study of linear codes over finite rings has received much attention in the last decades 
of the twentieth century. A linear code whose codewords are invariant under a circular shift 
is known as a cyclic code. A cyclic code of length n over a ring R is an ideal of the quo -
tient ring R[x]∕⟨xn − 1⟩ , and each codeword is identified by a polynomial of degree at most 
n − 1 . Due to such algebraic structure, the class of cyclic codes is a better choice among 
researchers for encoding and decoding purposes than other linear codes [ 1, 5]. In 1994, 
Hammons et al. [ 9] gave a new direction to linear codes over ℤ4 and obtained some good 
non-linear binary codes as the Gray images of linear codes. Afterwards, the study of linear 
codes over finite rings has gained significant attention from the coding analysts.

noncommutative rings have been attracting many researchers, we refer [2, 8, 11–13, 15]. In 
fact, in 1997, the first concrete set of alphabets A = M2(𝔽2) appeared in algebraic construc-
tions of modular lattices [3]. Later, in 2012, Oggier et  al. [12] developed the theory for 
noncommutative ring M2(𝔽2) in the context of space-time codes. In 2013, Alahmadi et al. 
[2] studied cyclic codes over the same ring M2(𝔽2) . In 2014, F alcunit and Sison [7] dis-
cussed the structure of cyclic codes over M2(𝔽p) and obtained the isometric images of these 
codes over the ring 𝔽p2 + u𝔽p2 . Mor eover, in 2018, Luo and Udaya [11] presented the struc-
ture of cyclic codes over the matrix ring M2(𝔽2 + u𝔽2) and obtained some optimal codes of 
even length over 𝔽4 . A gain, in 2018, Bhowmick et al. [4] discussed cyclic codes and their 
dual over a matrix ring M2(ℤ4) . F urther, in 2019, Pal et al. [13] studied cyclic codes over 
the matrix ring M4(𝔽2) . R ecently, Islam et al. [10] extended the work of [11] to the ring 
M2(𝔽p + u𝔽p) , where p ≡ 2 or 3 (mod 5) and studied cyclic codes of length n.

7  Conclusion
In 2019, the structure of cyclic codes over M4(𝔽2) was discussed by Pal et al. [13] for odd 
length. Initially, we have extended their work to the matrix ring R = M4(𝔽2 + u𝔽2) and 
derived the structure theorem for cyclic codes of odd length. Then we have obtained the 
images of cyclic codes of odd length over the ring R under 𝜙 as binary quasi-cyclic codes 
of even length over M4(𝔽2) . Based on der ived results, we have provided some good lin-
ear codes over 𝔽16 . Her e, a natural question comes to generalizing this study of codes of 
even length over R and finding their generators. It is still open and we can carry forward 
this research to both the Euclidean and the Hermitian self-dual cyclic codes over the ring 
R = M4(𝔽2 + u𝔽2).
