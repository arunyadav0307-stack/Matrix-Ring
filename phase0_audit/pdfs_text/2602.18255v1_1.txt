1.Introduction
Prange [16] introduced cyclic codes in 1957 and has attracted the attention
of several researchers since their elaboration. It is an important class of linear
codes and plays a vital role in developing error-correcting codes. Researchers have
done numerous revolutionary works on cyclic codes over finite fields and finite
commutative rings. Furthermore, many well-known codes, such as BCH, Kerdock,
Golay, Reed-Muller, Preparata, Justesen, and binary Hamming codes, are cyclic
or derived from it. A cyclic code of lengthnover a finite fieldFis the ideal of
the quotient ringF[x]/⟨x n −1⟩. Being rich with algebraic properties and ease
of implementation, researchers prefer this class of codes for encoding and decoding
over traditional linear codes. In 1994, Hammons et al. [10] provided a new direction
for linear codes overZ 4 and produced several good nonlinear binary codes as Gray
images of linear codes. Subsequently, researchers have shown interest in studying
linear codes over finite rings.
Aside from some notable works on finite commutative rings, codes on non-
commutative rings have lately drawn the attention of many researchers; we refer
[2, 8, 12, 13, 14, 18]. In fact, in algebraic constructions of modular lattices [3],
the first concrete set of alphabetsA=M 2(F2) appeared in 1997. Later, Oggier

2.Motivation and Background
Motivated by [13], here we consider an extension of this setting by studying
cyclic codes over the matrix ringR. In the context of slow fading MIMO (Multi-
ple Input Multiple Output) channels, as considered in the [13] on space-time coded
modulations, robust code design is essential for reliable communication. Such chan-
nels benefit significantly from codes that ensure full diversity and a non-vanishing
determinant. Paper demonstrated that when the inner code has a cyclic algebra
structure over a number field, as for perfect space-time codes, an outer code can be
designed via coset coding, more precisely, by taking the quotient of the algebra by
a two-sided ideal, which leads to matrices over finite alphabets for the outer code.
Hence, codes over the matrix ringM 4(F2), when properly structured, offer strong
candidates for space-time code design. Specifically, they exploited the cyclic alge-
braic structure ofM 4(F2), using the isomorphism withF 16 +eF 16 +e 2F16 +e 3F16,
wheree 4 = 1, to define an outer code structure.
On the other hand, our considered ringRinherits a similar algebraic structure
but enriches the space with additional nilpotent elements, offering a larger alphabet
and potential for increased flexibility in code design. Our structure can similarly
be viewed as a module over a non-commutative ring with a cyclic base, as used
in the case ofM 4(F2). We propose that analogous determinant conditions can be
developed for codes overM 4(F2[u]/⟨uk⟩), though this remains an open problem.
The study of such codes thus aims not only to generalize the known results over
F16, but also to potentially uncover new families of codes suitable for space-time
coded modulation in slow fading MIMO channels.

4.Dual of Cyclic Codes overR
In the study of linear codes over finite rings or fields, we define important struc-
tural properties such as self-orthogonality, dual-containing, and self-duality for a
chosen inner product. A codeCis said to be self-orthogonal ifC ⊆ C ⊥, dual-
containing ifC ⊥ ⊆ C, and self-dual ifC=C ⊥, whereC ⊥ denotes the dual ofC.
These notions depend on the type of dual being considered. Studying these duals
is particularly important because codes that are self-orthogonal or dual-containing
with respect to these inner products play a fundamental role in constructing quan-
tum error-correcting codes. Specifically, in the Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) and
Hermitian constructions of quantum codes, one typically starts with a classical code
satisfying these inclusion properties, which ensures that the resulting quantum code
has the necessary orthogonality conditions for error correction in quantum chan-
nels. In our case, we are studying the structure of such duals. However, the precise
characterization of self-orthogonal, dual-containing, and self-dual codes remains an
open problem over the considered algebraic structure.

7.Conclusion
In 2022, Patel et al. [15] discussed the structure of cyclic codes overM4(F2+uF2).
[15] for odd length. Initially, we have extended their work to the matrix ring
R=M 4(F2 +uF 2 +· · ·+uk−1F2). Then, we have derived the structure of cyclic
codes, which includes the formation of ideals of the ringR, cyclic codes as direct
sums of submodules, and the cardinality of cyclic codes over the ringR, respectively.
Here, we have studied the Euclidean and Hermitian duals of the derived cyclic codes.
Further, in the module isometry for the given ring, we have defined the Bachoc map
and the Gray map, which takes our cyclic code overRtoF 16. In addition, we have
provided some non-trivial examples of linear codes overF 16 with good parameters
that support our derived results, as well as a comparison of some existing codes for
the structureM 4(F2) in [14] and obtained codes overF 16 for the extended structure

29
M4(F2[u]/⟨uk⟩). Here, the study of self-dual, self-orthogonal, and dual-containing
codes overRwill be an interesting problem to be discussed. One can also attempt
to derive a deterministic criterion which will prove that our extended family of
codes is also a good choice for transmission through the MIMO channel, as given
in the case ofM 4(F2) in [13].
