Over M3(Fp + uFp + u2Fp)
Shikha Patel 1, Soham Ravikant Joshi 2 and Om Prakash 3

Abstract—Let Fp be a finite field with p elements and
Fp[u]/⟨u3⟩ = Fp + uFp + u2Fp, u3 = 0 be a finite chain ring.
This paper presents the construction of cyclic codes over Fp3 for
certain primes (p ≤ 100) from a noncommutative non-chain ring
R = M3(Fp[u]/⟨u3⟩) of 3 × 3 matrices. First, we show that the
structure of R is isomorphic to (Fp3 +eFp3 +e2Fp3 +u(Fp3 +eFp3 +
e2Fp3 )+ u2(Fp3 +eFp3 +e2Fp3 ) where e3 = 1, u3 = 0, uiej = ejui
for i ∈ {1, 2} and j ∈ {1, 2, 3}. Then we provide the form of ideals
of R and derive that cyclic codes can be written as the direct sum
of R-submodules of R[x] <xn−1> . Further, we find the formula for the cardinality of cyclic codes over R. Later, in the module isometry
for R, we define the Gray map, which takes derived cyclic codes
over R to Fp3. In addition, we provide non-trivial examples of
linear codes over Fp3 with good parameters which support our
derived results.
Index Terms—Finite field, cyclic codes, matrix rings, Gray map,
MDS/AMDS codes.
I. I NTRODUCTION
The study of cyclic codes over finite rings has received con-
siderable attention in the last decades of the twentieth century
and has made significant contributions to the development of
error-correcting codes. Several studies have been conducted
over the past three decades on cyclic codes over finite fields and
finite commutative rings. Many well-known codes, including
binary Hamming codes, Reed-Muller, Golay, Kerdock, BCH,
Preparata, and Justesen codes, are either cyclic or constructed
from cyclic codes. Recall that a cyclic code of length n over a
finite field Fq is also classified as the ideal of the quotient ring
Fq[x]/⟨xn − 1⟩. Due to the rich algebraic structure, researchers
prefer the class of cyclic codes for encoding and decoding
purposes over conventional linear codes.
To obtain a new code with better parameters, cyclic codes
over finite commutative rings were investigated in [13], [15],
[16], [18]. Matrix rings also produce distinct and good cyclic
codes, much like skew polynomial rings. This was accom-
plished by Alahmadi et al. [1], who obtained different self-dual
F4-codes and cyclic codes structure over M2(F2) concerning
Euclidean self-dual cyclic values. In 2014, Falcunit and Sison
[5] further investigated the algebraic properties of cyclic codes
over a more extended ring M2(Fp), where p ≡ 2 or 3 (mod 5) .
Furthermore, Luo and Udaya [9] investigated cyclic codes over
the matrix ring M2(F2[u]/⟨u2⟩) in 2018. Several quaternary
optimal codes were obtained by using an isometry between
M2(F2+uF2) and F4[u, v]/⟨u2, v2, uv−vu⟩. Later, Bhowmick
et al. [2] studied self-dual cyclic codes over M2(Z4), and Pal
et al. [12] studied cyclic codes over M4(F2). The cyclic codes
over M2(Fp + uFp) were also examined by Islam et al. [7]
in 2021. Further, in 2022, Patel et al. [14] discussed cyclic
codes over M4(F2 + uF2) and obtained many good codes over
F16. More recently, in [8], the authors considered the cyclic
codes over M3(Fp) in 2024 and discovered good linear codes
over F8 for p = 2. Continuing with that framework, we find
linear codes over different fields with better parameters. Two
significant contributions of the paper are given below.
• We analyze and explore the algebraic structure of the
noncommutative non-chain ring M3(Fp + uFp + u2Fp)
with u3 = 0.
• We obtain some linear codes which are either MDS
(maximum-distance-separable) or AMDS (almost
maximum-distance-separable).
The paper is organized as follows: Section II establishes the
structure of R = M3(Fp + uFp + u2Fp) while Section
III studies the cyclic code on R as the direct sum of the
submodules of R. Section IV defines the Gray map for the
correspondence between F9 p3 and R, and Section V includes
some examples of non-trivial codes to support our obtained
results. Finally, Section VI concludes the paper.
II. C ONSTRUCTION OF M3(Fp + uFp + u2Fp)
Let Fp be the finite field of p elements. Throughout the
paper, R represents the ring M3(Fp[u]/⟨u3⟩), which is a finite
noncommutatie non-chain ring. For a prime p ≤ 100, let
f (x) ∈ Fp[x], an irreducible polynomial of degree 3. For
example, f (x) = x3 − x − 1 is irreducible in Fp[x] for
p ∈ {2, 3, 13, 29, 31, 41, 47, 71, 73} as it has no roots in Fp.
Similarly, g(x) = x3 + x + 1 is irreducible in Fp[x] for
p ∈ {2, 5, 7, 19, 41, 59, 71, 97} and h(x) = x3 + 3x + 2 is
irreducible in Fp[x] for p ∈ {5, 7, 11, 31, 53, 73, 83, 97}. We
can choose any of f (x), g(x), or h(x) depending on which
prime we have to construct Fp3 = Fp[ω] which is a Galois
field where f (ω) = 0 or g(ω) = 0 or h(ω) = 0 . The Galois
group G = Gal(Fp3 /Fp) is cyclic generated by Frobenius
automorphism σ : Fp3 − →Fp3 defined by σ(a) = ap. Also,
using the construction given in [8], [11], we have a cyclic
algebra A = ( Fp3 /Fp, σ,1) = Fp3 + eFp3 + e2Fp3 where
ae = eσ(a), and e3 = 1 for all a ∈ Fp3. Here, A is a central

V. E XAMPLES
A linear q-ary [n, k, d] code is said to be MDS (maximum
distance separable) if d = n − k + 1, i.e., if it meets the
Singleton bound d ≤ n − k + 1. One of the basic motives
in the construction of an [n, k] linear code is to optimize d and
try to achieve dM DS= n − k + 1. A code is said to be AMDS
(almost MDS) if its minimum distance d = n − k, and it is
optimal if it attains the highest possible minimum distance for
its given length and dimension. For n = 3 and 5, following
is the construction strategy of cyclic codes over F125, F27, and
F8, which are the Gray images of cyclic codes. We determine

VI. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, we have extended the work of [8] to the matrix
ring R = M3(Fp+uFp+u2Fp). We have deduced the structure
of cyclic codes, which includes the cardinality of cyclic codes
over the ring R, the formation of ideals of the ring R, and
cyclic codes as direct sums of submodules. Additionally, we
have defined the Gray map for the specified ring, which takes
cyclic code over R to Fp3. Furthermore, we have presented a
few non-trivial examples of linear codes over F125, F27, and F8
with good parameters that validate the results we have derived.
