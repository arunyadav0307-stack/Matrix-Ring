Abstract
Let Fp be the ﬁnite ﬁeld with p elements where p is a prime, and R b ea3 ×3 matrices
over Fp[u]/⟨uk⟩, i.e., R = M3(Fp + uFp +···+ uk−1Fp) with uk = 0. First, we
study the structure of cyclic codes over R as the direct sum of submodules of R, along
with the Euclidean and Hermitian dual for the ringS = M3(F3 +uF3 +···+ uk−1F3).
Later, to show that the structure is suitable for the construction of good linear codes,
we provide many AMDS (almost maximum-distance-separable) and best-known lin-
ear codes over F125, F27, and F8. Further, we study dual-containing codes over S
using a new non-degenerate inner product and provide a new approach to constructing
quantum codes over matrix rings via the CSS (Calderbank-Shor-Steane) construction.
Finally, we construct several quantum codes with small quantum Singleton defects
using a suitable Gray map.

Further, cyclic codes have been extensively investigated over both ﬁnite commutative

codes over a more extended ring M2(Fp) where p ≡ 2o r3 (mod 5), were stud-

matrix ring M2(F2[u]/⟨u2⟩) were studied by Luo & Udaya [ 21], in which an isome-

various quaternary optimal codes. In 2024, Islam et al. [ 17] studied the cyclic codes
over M3(Fp), where they found many good cyclic codes over F8. In 2022, Patel et
al. [ 26] studied cyclic codes over M4(F2 + uF2) and recently in 2025, Joshi et al.
[19] extended this work to the construction of new codes over F16 from cyclic codes
over M4(F2 + uF2 + u2F2), whereas Patel et al. [ 25] extended the work of [ 17]t o
the construction of codes over Fp3 from cyclic codes over M3(Fp + uFp + u2Fp).
Motivated by these works, we extend the work of [ 17] to the more general structure
M3(Fp + uFp +···+ uk−1Fp) to see whether the structure is suitable for the con-
struction of linear codes over F125 and F27, along with F8 for more generalized length
3kn . These studies not only deepen the understanding of cyclic codes over the matrix
rings but also indicate potential applications beyond classical coding theory, including
quantum error correction, where dual-containing cyclic codes play an important role.
In classical information theory, error correction occurs in bits, whereas in quan-
tum information theory, error correction occurs in quantum states (qubits). Both are
concerned with the fundamental problem of reliable transmission of information over
a noisy channel. However, quantum error-correcting codes (QECCs) protect quan-
tum information from errors caused by decoherence and other types of quantum
noise. Shor [31] in 1995, and Steane [32] in 1996 independently constructed quantum
error-correcting codes from classical ones. In 1998, Calderbank et al. [ 6] proposed a
systematic method to obtain quantum codes from classical ones, popularly known as
the CSS (Calderbank-Shor-Steane) construction. In recent years, the construction of
quantum codes via the CSS method has continued to beneﬁt from advances in dual-
containing classical codes. In particular, researchers have used cyclic, quasi-cyclic,
skew-cyclic, and constacyclic codes and their dual-containing properties to construct
quantum codes with improved or competitive parameters via the CSS construction.
In 2009, Qian et al. [30] provided a construction for quantum error-correcting codes
from cyclic codes over the ﬁnite ring R = F2 + uF2 and obtained inﬁnite families
of quantum error-correcting codes. In 2011, Kai and Zhu [ 20] studied quantum codes
from cyclic codes over R = F4 + uF4, where u2 = 0. Dertli et al. [ 8] in 2015 studied
the quantum codes obtained from cyclic codes overA2 = F2+uF2+vF2+uvF2, u2 =
u,v 2 = v, uv = vu. Later, in 2020, Islam et al. [ 18] studied the quantum codes from

for this work are as follows.
(1) In [ 17], authors have studied the cyclic codes over M3(Fp) and found many good
linear codes over F8 with length 3 n such that p ∤ n. In this paper, we extend this
work to the study of cyclic codes over R = M3(Fp + uFp +···+ uk−1Fp) to
get linear codes over more generalized length, which is 3 kn where p ∤ n and also
study whether this structure is capable of producing good linear codes over F125
for p = 5, F27 for p = 3, along with F8 for p = 2.
(2) We propose a novel approach for constructing quantum codes using the CSS con-
struction, leveraging cyclic codes over non-chain, noncommutative matrix rings.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the ﬁrst study in the literature that explores
quantum code construction from such matrix rings. Speciﬁcally, we investigate
dual-containing codes over the ring S = M3(F3 + uF3 +···+ uk−1F3), and
utilize a suitable Gray map to obtain dual-containing linear codes over F27. These
linear codes are then employed in the CSS framework to construct quantum codes
with desirable parameters.
Moreover, an advantage of having length 3kn over 3n is that it is not possible to get
cyclic codes of some lengths using the ring M3(Fp). For example, p = 3, cyclic codes
of length 18 over F27 cannot be found by [ 17], as 3 n = 18 implies n = 6, which is
not possible. However, we can obtain cyclic codes of length 18 by setting 3 kn where
n = 2 and k = 3. Concrete examples of these cyclic codes of length 18 over F27 are
given in Sect. 5.2.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows: Sect. 2 provides the construction of
R = M3(Fp +uFp +···+ uk−1Fp) and deﬁnes the Gray map for the correspondence
between Fp3 and R. Section 3 studies the cyclic code over R as the direct sum of
submodules of R, while Sect. 4 discusses the Euclidean and Hermitian duals of the
ring S = M3(F3 + uF3 +···+···+ uk−1F3). Section 5 includes some examples of
non-trivial linear codes over F125, F27, and F8, whereas Sect. 6 studies the quantum
codes via the CSS construction, including the CSS code framework and deﬁnes a
new associated Gray map. Section 7 provides many new quantum codes with better
parameters, while Sect. 8 concludes the paper.

4 Dual of cyclic codes over M3(F3 + uF3 + u2F3 +···+ uk−1F3)
In the study of linear codes over ﬁnite rings or ﬁelds with a chosen inner product,
we deﬁne important structural properties such as self-orthogonality, dual-containing,
and self-duality. A code C is said to be self-orthogonal if C ⊆ C⊥, dual-containing
if C⊥ ⊆ C, and self-dual if C = C⊥, where C⊥ denotes the dual of C. Studying
these duals is particularly important because codes that are self-orthogonal or dual-
containing play a fundamental role in constructing quantum error-correcting codes. In
our case, we are studying the structure of such duals for the ring S = M3(F3 + uF3 +
u2F3 +···+ uk−1F3).

6.1 CSS code framework
Calderbank, Shor, and Steane [7, 32] proposed a framework for constructing quantum
error correction codes over qubits from two classical binary codesC1 and C2 satisfying
C⊥ 1 ⊆ C2. This class of codes are called the Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) codes.
The condition C⊥ 1 ⊆ C2 is called the dual-containing condition of CSS codes. By
considering the two codes C1 and C2 to be the same code, i.e., C1 = C2, we can
construct quantum codes from dual-containing classical codes as C⊥ 1 ⊆ C2 = C1.
The CSS codes form a class of stabilizer codes. Let H1 and H2 be the parity
check matrices of the classical codes C1[n, K1, d1] and C2[n, K2, d2], respectively.

8 Conclusion
In 2024, Islam et al. [17] discussed the structure of cyclic codes overM3(Fp), in which
they provided many good linear codes over F8 with length 3 n where p ∤ n. Initially,
we have extended their work and studied the structure of cyclic codes over the ring R
along with the Gray map, which takes our cyclic code over R to Fp3 , with length 3kn ,
where p ∤ n. We have shown that our structure is suitable for the construction of linear
codes overF125, F27,a sw e l la sF8. Further, along with Euclidean and Hermitian duals,
we have studied the dual-containing codes over the ring S. Using a suitable Gray map,
we have provided a novel method to study quantum codes over matrix rings via the
CSS construction. Finally, we have provided many better quantum codes to validate
our results. Here, the construction of self-dual and quantum codes over all possible
primes remains open and presents an interesting problem for further study.
